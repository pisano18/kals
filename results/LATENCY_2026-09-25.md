# Is speed costing the live bot money? -- 2026-09-25

Source: the bot's own live logs (`results/pinrun-live-2026*.jsonl`, `order`
records), the public trade tape (`C:\kals\kalshi_data\trade`, read-only) for
exchange timestamps of our own fills and of rival buyers, market results from
`market_lifecycle_v2` `determined` events (cross-checked: 888 of 888 agree
with the bot's own `settled` records), a read-only network probe to
`api.elections.kalshi.com`, and `w32tm` (query only). No replay. All times UTC.

Primary window: orders sent 2026-09-20 00:00Z .. 2026-09-25 07:00Z --
**412 orders, 217 closes, 5.28 days, 78 orders/day**. The ms-precision send
time (`t_ms_send`) exists on 251 of them (from 2026-09-22 12Z); 205 of those
fall in hours the trade tape covers (tape hours missing: 09-23 00, 01, 16, 18,
20-23, 09-24 00; 09-23 19 truncated). Extended window for power: 2026-09-08 ..
09-25, 1,216 orders with `latency_ms`, 716 closes (pre-09-19 records carry no
`want` or `ask_seen`; side inferred from `body.side`, money not computed).

## Plain-language conclusion

* **Speed costs a little, not a lot.** Arriving 50-80 ms earlier -- what a
  server next to the exchange would buy -- would have picked up about
  **$2-4 a day** more in this sample, and that is the best case, because every
  extra contract it would have caught happened to win. At the bot's measured
  loss rate on fills (about 2 losses in 100), an average extra contract is
  worth about zero, so the realistic value is **somewhere between -$1 and
  +$4 a day**.
* **Being faster also catches the offers that makers are pulling, and those
  are the losers.** Of 7 missed orders where nobody else bought (the seller
  pulled the offer), 6 would have won $2.51 in total and 1 would have lost
  **$77** (DOGE, 2026-09-23 02:44Z, 82 contracts at 93.4c). How many of those
  a faster bot would catch is not measured.
* **Half the gain is free, without a server.** The bot opens a brand-new
  encrypted connection for every order (`urllib.urlopen` in
  `ordercli.send`), which costs two extra round trips to Ohio (~43 ms) before
  the order even leaves. Measured from here: fresh connection + request
  90 ms, reused connection 23 ms. Keeping one connection open would cut
  ~40 of the ~82 ms it now takes our order to reach the exchange.
* **The orders we miss are not better than the ones we get.** Since 09-20 the
  unfilled contracts on winning markets were worth $95 of missed profit; the
  unfilled contracts on 2 losing markets would have lost $125. Not filling
  *saved* $30 net over 5.3 days. Over 17 days: fills lose 26 of 960 orders
  (2.7%), misses 11 of 279 (3.9%).
* **Clocks are fine.** Our fills print on the exchange 53-210 ms after we send
  (median 82), never before our send and never after our reply came back, so
  our clock and Kalshi's agree to within ~10-15 ms. Windows syncs every ~9 h;
  offset right now is 1-6 ms against three public time servers.
* **The evening is the slow time.** 23:00-02:00 UTC (19:00-22:00 ET) order
  round trips run p90 170-510 ms against ~130 ms the rest of the day, and fill
  66 of 100 orders versus 78 of 100 otherwise. Whether that is home internet
  or evening competition is not separable from these data.

## 5. Clock health

**Our own fills on the exchange clock.** 129 of 182 filled orders with a ms
send time (the rest mostly fall in hours the tape lacks) matched to exactly one group of trade prints whose
contracts sum to our fill (9 had more than one candidate; nearest to mid-flight
taken).

| exchange print time minus our send time | min | p10 | p50 | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ms | 53 | 63 | 82 | 118 | 210 |

* 0 prints before our send, 0 after our reply came back. Print time sits at
  0.79 of the round trip (p10 0.71, p90 0.85).
* Physical floor for the order to arrive: TCP + TLS + half a trip ~ 2.5 x
  19-21 ms ~ 50 ms. Minimum observed 53 ms. So the two clocks agree within
  roughly +/-10-15 ms.
* By day: minimum 71 / 64 / 53 / 58 ms on 09-22 / 23 / 24 / 25.

**Collector receipt floor** (p0.1 of local receipt minus exchange trade time,
1-in-25 sample): 16-17 ms on 09-22, 20-23 ms on 09-23, 25-30 ms 09-24 01-06Z,
19-20 ms 09-24 12Z on. Our clock drifts ~10-15 ms between syncs.

**Windows time (`w32tm /query /status`, 07:05Z):** source time.windows.com,
stratum 5, poll interval 32,768 s (~9 h), last sync 03:31Z, root dispersion
3.96 s (Windows' own loose error bound, not a measured error). Measured offset
(`w32tm /stripchart`, 4 samples each): time.windows.com -3 to -5 ms,
time.google.com -5 to -6 ms, time.cloudflare.com -1 to -6 ms.

Verdict: a 5-30 ms clock error is invisible to a bot that decides on
1-second prints and seconds-to-close. Not a problem.

## 6. What a server next to the exchange would be worth

**Time it would save.** Our order reaches the matching engine a median 82 ms
after we send (p90 118). Of that, ~50 ms is network (TCP + TLS + half a trip)
and ~30 ms is Kalshi-side or fresh-connection overhead that a VM may not
remove. Plus the book and index arrive ~10 ms sooner. Working range:
**50-80 ms earlier.**

**Direct count from the tape** (205 orders in tape-covered hours, 2.8 days,
67 not fully filled). For each, the contracts that *other* buyers took on our
side at or under our limit in the last N ms before our order arrived -- the
contracts an earlier arrival could have taken first -- capped at what we left
unfilled, valued at the real result:

| arrive earlier by | orders that gain | contracts | $ winners | $ losers | net $ | scaled to 78 orders/day | 95% range (by close) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 ms | 4 | 48 | $0.95 | $0 | +$0.95 | +$0.36/day | +$0.01 to +$0.87 |
| 25 ms | 10 | 195 | $3.93 | $0 | +$3.93 | +$1.50/day | +$0.20 to +$3.02 |
| 50 ms | 14 | 227 | $4.56 | $0 | +$4.56 | +$1.74/day | +$0.40 to +$3.39 |
| 80 ms | 23 | 469 | $11.05 | $0 | +$11.05 | +$4.20/day | +$1.77 to +$6.75 |
| 120 ms | 39 | 995 | $21.77 | $0 | +$21.77 | +$8.28/day | +$4.40 to +$11.66 |

(The range column is per calendar day of the 2.78-day span; the scaled column
uses 78 orders/day.)

Assumptions and what breaks them:

1. **Every gained contract won.** 60 of 60 missed orders that lost a race to
   another buyer were on winning markets. At 97.5c a contract makes 2.3c when
   it wins and loses 97.7c when it loses; break-even is ~2.3 losses per 100.
   Our fills lose 1.9 per 100 (09-20+, 6 of 313) to 2.7 per 100 (09-08+, 26 of
   960). At those rates the 80 ms gain (~178 contracts/day) is worth
   **+$0.8/day to -$0.6/day** in expectation. The +$4.20 is the realized,
   lucky-side figure.
2. **Offers pulled by the seller.** 7 missed orders had no rival buyer -- the
   offer was withdrawn. 6 were on winners ($2.51 total), 1 on a loser
   (-$76.94). A faster arrival catches some of these before the cancel; how
   many is not measured (needs `orderbook_delta`, 183 MB/hour, not read).
   Catching the one loser erases 18 days of the 80 ms gain.
3. Rivals' timing is assumed unchanged; nobody else is assumed to beat us.
4. Orders the bot never sent because the offer was gone before it decided
   are not in this count.

**Cross-check from our own latency spread:** -1.0 pts of fill per +10 ms
(17 days) x 80 ms ~ +8 pts, but that slope comes from slow orders and the
74-130 ms range is flat, so it overstates the gain below 74 ms.

**Estimate: a ~1 ms server is worth -$1 to +$4 a day for speed alone, most
likely about +$1-2, before its own cost.** About half of it (the two
handshake round trips, ~40 ms) is available from home at no cost by reusing
one connection for orders. The speed case alone does not justify the server;
the disk/uptime case may.

## Not measured

* Maker-cancel races (assumption 2) -- the largest uncertainty, and it
  points toward speed costing money on losers.
* Hedge orders (41 records) -- not analysed; they use the same fresh-connection
  path.
* Offers taken before the bot decided (never sent).
