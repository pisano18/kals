## 1. F-B05 x F-I01 — does the calm STATE persist close-to-close?

- **F-B05** — the loss brake counts losing CLOSES; three stops the run for the ET day.
- **F-I01** — the settlement index arrives once per second.

**Measurement.** From `cfbenchmarks_value` alone: per coin-close, the 300 s
choppiness ruler `pinflood` already uses, cut into fifths once over the whole
sample, then the lag-1 transition matrix between consecutive closes of the same
coin. Two things fall out. (a) The persistence of the calmest fifth implies a
miss-after-miss lift, via F-M12's 4.28%/1.05% conditional rates; compare it with
F-M10's measured 0.86x [0.55, 1.36]. Appreciable persistence makes those two
facts incompatible and one of them an artefact — the usual way this project
finds bugs. (b) It is the independence the three-closes-a-day brake rests on: if
bad closes cluster by regime, a per-day counter is the wrong shape and a
state-conditional size cut is the cheaper guard.

**Artefact.** Chiefly the clock: volatility has a diurnal cycle and multi-hour
regimes, so choppiness is autocorrelated for reasons nothing can trade.
Re-measure after residualising choppiness on hour-of-day within coin and report
both numbers; F-M11 says the hour does not predict the miss, but it certainly
predicts calmness. Second: use the 300 s ruler only, so adjacent windows do not
overlap. A 1800 s or 3600 s ruler (AMENDMENT 20) shares seconds between
neighbouring closes and manufactures persistence by construction.

**MDE, stated first.** ~18,178 coin-closes is ~1,515 closes, ~1,850 effective
observations at rho 0.8 (F-R01). Lag-1 correlation of choppiness: callable above
|r| ~ 0.07. Calm-to-calm transition: baseline 0.20, ~370 effective calm closes,
callable above ~0.26. Below those, the answer is "no power", not "no effect".

**Cost.** Cheap. Index only — no `load_quotes`, so no RAM contest with the
collector. One pass plus its self-test; under an hour.
