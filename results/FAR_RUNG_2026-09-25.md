# FAR_RUNG_2026-09-25 -- buying the far rungs of the hourly ladder at 99c

Written 2026-09-25 from 03:0xZ. Read-only measurement: the index tape
(`C:\kals\kalshi_data\cfbenchmarks_value`, streamed), live GETs through
`research/kauth.py`, and the live bot's own logs. No order was placed, no
process was touched, nothing under `kalshi_data` or `feed_data` was written.

The idea (plain): on the hourly BTC ladder (`KXBTCD`, one event an hour, 188
rungs $100 apart) a rung that is $300 or more from the index with 45 seconds
left is almost certain to finish on the side it is on -- the settlement is the
average of the last 60 one-second prints, and 15 of them are already known.
Those rungs trade at 99c with thousands of contracts resting. A winning 99c
contract nets about 0.93c after the fee; a losing one costs about 99c. So
the idea lives or dies on ONE number: how often a rung at a given cushion is
crossed. Break-even is **0.93 crossings per 100 contracts** (0.9307c won /
100.0007c swing).

The watcher (`research/rungwatch.py`, pid 2852332, started 03:02:24Z) takes
its first poll at 03:58:30Z. This section was written before that poll.

**The cushion C** is fixed from part 1 (the index alone) before part 2 is
read: C = the smallest of {$100, $150, $200, $300, $500} at which, at tau 45,
the settlement moved that far from the projection on ZERO of the closes
since 2026-09-01 (hourly AND quarter-hour closes pooled, two-sided). If no
band is zero, the idea stops here and no test is proposed.

**The live test** (if proposed): 7 days, 1 contract per rung, at most one rung
per side per close, only rungs at cushion >= C at tau <= 45 s, only at
<= 99c, only on `KXBTCD`. PASS requires ALL of:

1. **>= 100 filled contracts** counted over >= 30 closes (rule 4: closes, not
   fills). Fewer means the supply is not what the books show; extend once by
   7 days, then stop.
2. **ZERO crossings** on any rung we filled, AND zero crossings at cushion >=
   C on the index tape over the same 7 days (the tape is re-run daily; the
   population that matters is ours, but the tape is the earlier warning).
   One crossing = KILL, because one 99c loss needs 107 wins to earn back.
3. **Per-contract net >= +0.8c after fee**, computed as (wins x 0.93c -
   losses x 99.07c) / contracts, from Kalshi's own fee field on each fill.
   With zero losses this is 0.93c; the 0.8c floor leaves room for the fee
   ceiling (`billed_fee` rounds each ORDER's fee up to the next $0.0001: a
   1-contract order at 99c bills $0.0007 = 0.07c, so size 1 nets 0.93c).
   *Corrected 03:2xZ, before any data: the first draft of this line said the
   1-contract fee rounds to 1c and the net is zero; that was wrong by 14x
   (the 1c-per-contract case is size 0.01, per pinrun.net_edge's docstring).
   The 0.8c number did not move.*
4. **Fill rate >= 50%** of orders sent (the 15-minute bot fills 70%; the
   hourly 1-contract test has no fill record yet).
5. **The supply is still there**: the watcher's median contracts at <= 99c
   in the C band at tau 45 stays >= 500 per close over the test week.

FAIL any one and the idea stops; nothing here moves after the numbers are

## 1. How often is a rung crossed? -- the index tape alone (2026-09-01 00:00Z to 09-25 03:00Z)

**Source.** The recorder's own one-per-second BRTI prints, 561 hour files
streamed (never the growing current hour); 18 hour files are missing and 21
stop at a corrupt gzip block (mostly 09-02/09-03), so **2,136 quarter-hour
closes** have all 60 settlement prints on disk (93% of the 2,304 possible
over 24 days), **533 of them hourly**. Script:
`scratchpad/far_part1.py` (self-test plants a $200 drop and a flat world;
refuses real data on a red test; no replay import).

**The arithmetic checks out three ways.** The 60-print mean computed here
equals the exchange's own 60-second average carried on the print AT the
close on **2,133 of 2,133** closes where it is present; the 09-11 14:00Z
close was recomputed by hand from the raw prints with plain JSON parsing and
matched to the cent (+$95.31); and the 09-19 20:00Z loss below reproduces
the live bot's own numbers.

**Definitions.** With `tau` seconds left, *projection* = (the 60-tau prints
already locked + tau copies of the current print) / 60 -- exactly what the
bot's `fair()` sees. A rung $c from the projection is *crossed* when the
settlement ends more than $c on the far side of it. Counts below are
two-sided (a move of more than $c in EITHER direction), which overstates the
risk to any ONE rung by about 2x; that is the conservative direction.

The two closes beyond $100 at 45 s: **09-11 12:30Z** ($136; the index

### What a 99c contract is worth, by cushion (BTC, 45 s)

A winning 99c contract nets **0.93c** (1c minus the 0.07c fee); a losing
one costs **99.07c**. Break-even is **0.93 crossings per 100 contracts**.
"Worst case" is the highest crossing rate the sample cannot rule out (95%).

| cushion | crossed, of 2,136 | rate | worth per contract | worst-case rate | worth at worst case |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| >$50 | 15 | 0.70% (1 in 142) | +0.23c | 1.08% | **-0.15c** |
| >$100 | 2 | 0.09% (1 in 1,068) | +0.84c | 0.29% | +0.64c |
| **>$150** | **0** | 0 | +0.93c | 0.14% (1 in 713) | +0.79c |
| >$200 | 0 | 0 | +0.93c | 0.14% | +0.79c |
| >$300 | 0 | 0 | +0.93c | 0.14% | +0.79c |

**The pre-registered rule in section 0 therefore fixes C = $150** (the
smallest band with zero crossings at 45 s). At 30 s the same table says
$150 (one $101 miss); at 15 s even $50 was never crossed (worst $45).

**What this number is NOT.** It is the index, not our fills. At a $150
cushion the seller cannot "know" the 60-second average -- 15 of the 60
prints are already fixed and the other 45 would have to average $200 away
from the current print. The adverse stories left are: (a) a bad print or
feed outage on CF Benchmarks' side (none in 24 days: the exchange average
matched the prints 2,133/2,133); (b) a day 3x worse than 09-11 (whose $432
minute-range was the sample's worst; a $1,300 minute would cross $300); (c)
a maker resting 99c on both sides for the fee-free 1c, which is harmless to
us. The sample is 24 days and contains no flash crash. That is the honest
size of the claim.

## 3. The bank constraint

Rule from `results/SCALING_PLAN_2026-09-24.md`: one worst-case close may
cost 25% of the bank. Here the worst close is **one whole side** losing (a
rung $150 below the index bought YES and a rung $150 above bought NO cannot
both lose; a big move takes one side). Per side that is 0.25 x bank / $0.99
contracts. Each side wins 0.93c a contract when nothing crosses; 20 closes a
day (Kalshi lists no hourly event 02:00-05:00 ET).

| bank | contracts a side | both sides | made per close (no crossing) | made per day | one crossing costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $900 | 227 | 454 | $4.23 | **$85** | -$225 |
| $2,500 | 631 | 1,262 | $11.75 | $235 | -$625 |
| $5,000 | 1,262 | 2,524 | $23.49 | $470 | -$1,250 |
| $10,000 | 2,525 | 5,050 | $47.00 | $940 | -$2,502 |

Two honest caveats on that table. First, buying both sides puts 50% of the
bank into one close (25% a side); if the operator reads the 25% rule as
"everything at risk in the close", halve every row ($42/day at $900).
Second, the $900 is the same bank the 15-minute bot spends -- its per-close
budget today is 2 x 86 contracts at 98c = $169 plus an extra bet, and both
strategies fire on the same :00 close -- so the "usable" figure for the
ladder is nearer $900 minus ~$250 = $650 (182 a side, $68/day) until the
bank grows. The per-day figures assume the DEPTH exists at C at every close
and that our order actually fills, which is what part 2 and the live test
measure; a crossing rate of 0.14% (the worst the tape allows) turns $85/day
into about $72/day on average with a -$225 day roughly every 35 days.

## 4. What the bot would need (described, NOT implemented -- `arm-toxic` / v-tradefeed are live on pinrun.py)

The live bot already watches KXBTCD (v-btcd1, 1 contract) and in 10 hours
it has sent **zero** hourly orders: 70 refusals -- 33 `no_offer`, 26
`book_stale`, 6 `confidence`, and **5 `price_ceiling` at exactly 99c** (one
of them 7,619 contracts at 7 s, the others at 43-45 s with the model
99.6-100% sure). So the supply the operator saw is real and the 98c ceiling
is exactly what turns it away. Had the ceiling been 99c those five would
have died one line later at `ev_floor`: `expected_value(0.99)` with the
15-minute flip rate 0.9% is +0.03c, under the 0.3c floor. Every change below
is gated on `series_size_for(tk) is not None`, i.e. a ladder ticker, so
the 15-minute path is byte-identical:

1. **Universe by cushion, not nearest-3.** `ladder_universe()` keeps
   `LADDER_KEEP = 3` rungs nearest the spot -- the $0-100 rungs, which are
   the ones this idea must NOT buy. New flags `--ladder-cushion-min 150
   --ladder-cushion-max 600`: keep every rung whose |spot - K| is inside
   that window on BOTH sides (4-5 a side, ~9 tickers), computed on the
   projection when one exists. `LADDER_PAGE_MAX` already fetches the whole
   event (188 rows, one page at limit 200).
2. **Per-series price ceiling.** `SERIES_CEILING = None` (shipped),
   `--series-ceiling 0.99`; at the gate (`if price > PRICE_CEILING`, pinrun
   ~line 16606) use `SERIES_CEILING` when the ticker is a ladder ticker and
   the flag is set. `_DEFAULT_*` asserted in the self-test, running value
   never.
3. **Per-series flip rate and EV floor.** `expected_value(price,
   flip=MEASURED_FLIP)` bakes in the 15-minute pick-off rate. New
   `--series-flip 0.0014` (the worst rate the tape allows at C) and
   `--series-ev-floor 0.005`: at 99c that gives +0.79c against a 0.5c
   floor. The 15-minute `MEASURED_FLIP` is untouched.
4. **The cushion gate** -- the one that matters. Before the ceiling gate,
   for a ladder ticker only: `mu = (locked + r*spot)/60` (the projection
   `fair()` already computes; expose it) and refuse when `|mu - K| <
   SERIES_CUSHION_MIN` ($150) with the gate name `cushion` and `mu`, `K`,
   `tau` in the record. What it BLOCKS (written down, per the 09-19
   lesson): entries on ladder rungs only. It sits ABOVE the hedge pass and
   can never block a hedge. `tau <= 45` comes for free from the existing
   entry window (`TAU_MAX 30`, early leg 45).
5. **Its own per-close budget.** `MAX_PER_CLOSE = 2` counts MARKETS per
   close and the hourly close IS the :00 quarter close, so nine rungs would
   fight the twelve 15-minute coins for two slots (and `coin_of()` maps
   KXBTCD to KXBTC, so the extra-coin allowance does not help). v-btcd1
   skipped a separate budget deliberately because one rung costs 1 of 156;
   nine rungs do not. Needs `series_max_per_close` (a separate counter
   keyed on `close_s` for ladder tickers) so the ladder legs neither
   consume nor are refused by the 15-minute budget. `both_sides` is per
   MARKET (`prev["sides"][tk]`), so YES on a lower rung and NO on an upper
   rung do not collide.
6. **The 1-contract test path is v-btcd1's, unchanged:** `--series KXBTCD
   --series-size 1` (already refused live without `--series-size`), the cap
   re-applied after every widener, and a `v-farrung1` entry in
   `results/VERSIONS.md` at the moment of deploy with the exact revert
   (delete the six flags from `restart_bot.ps1`, run it).

Estimated size: ~120 lines of code plus ~80 of self-test (a driven world
where a 99c rung at $150 is taken at size 1, the same rung at $90 is
refused `cushion`, and a 15-minute market at 99c is still refused
`price_ceiling`), plus `sync_arms.ps1` since the file hash moves for every
arm. The order of the self-test checks must prove the cushion gate precedes
the send and follows the hedge pass, the way `pintake`'s rail order is
