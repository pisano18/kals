## The answer

Nothing this hunt found is ready to make money: 0 of the 120 ideas tested passed as strong (survived every check and pays at least $5 a day). Two protective rules passed, worth about $0 a day today: one keeps the live bots working if Kalshi removes the old "which side did the buyer take" field or flips how NO prices are shown, the other makes every future resting-order bot safe across the Thursday trading pause. The biggest unproven ideas ($12-50 a day on paper) all collect Kalshi's liquidity reward (money Kalshi pays people who keep orders resting in the book), and the only reward Kalshi has ever paid this account is the coin race ($11.04 from 3 races on 10-11, landing in the main account), so each is worth $0 until a free paper recorder, and then a small signed-off live test, shows we get paid and that our fills cost what the recordings say. The most useful find is about losing less: the live forecaster waits about 1.3 seconds after each index print by design, and the offers it missed by waiting made about +3 cents a contract (an upper bound), so a faster paper version is the first thing to run. 83 tested ideas are dead or refuted and 112 more were measured dead before testing, each with the number that killed it; none of this changes the live bots today.

Plain meanings of the few market words used below: a *maker* has an order resting in the book and waits to be filled; a *taker* buys or sells at a price already on offer; *the tape* is our own recording of Kalshi's books and trades; the *holdout* is data held back and never used to choose the rule; *fair* is our model's own price; *calibrated* means prices matched how often things really happened; a *4-sigma print* is an index tick about 4 times its usual one-second size; *bp* is a basis point, a hundredth of a percent.

## Lose-less leads for the live bots

These came out of the final critic's re-test of SH86 (scratch: `.sweep/hunt-2026-10-15/test-20261015-R3-M-14-RT2/`, files latdecomp.txt, inbound.py, lateprobe.py, fc_optreal.txt). They are measured on our own logs and recordings, not on the replay.

**A. The forecaster waits about 1.3 seconds by design, and the offers it loses by waiting were winners.**
- Its live order reaches Kalshi 1,215 / 1,259 / 1,296 ms (fastest tenth / middle / slowest tenth of 117 October orders) after the index print it decided on.
- Where the time goes: about 22 ms for the print to reach us; about 110 ms because the paper engine decides 0.2 s after the print's second (DECIDE_OFFSET 0.2 s in bettor_paper.py); about 1,000 ms because live only sends once the paper's cautious "still there 1 second later" fill rule (SURVIVE_S 1.0 s) has booked a fill, a paper safety rule copied into live; about 100 ms because each order opens a fresh connection to Kalshi.
- What that costs, on its own paper engine 09-29..10-16 (977 decisions, ~331 closes): filled at the moment of decision, 42,389 contracts at +2.09 cents a contract; filled only if still there 1 s later (what live does today), 10,304 contracts at -1.58 cents (give or take 3.65); the 76 in 100 contracts lost by waiting were +3.21-cent winners (give or take 1.49; first half +4.15, second half +2.26). This is an upper bound: it assumes we would be first about 110 ms after the print.
- Next test, paper only: a variant beside the live forecaster that decides when the print arrives (DECIDE_OFFSET 0), counts a fill only if the offer is still shown 75 ms later, and keeps one connection open. Bar, written before the read: 200+ paper fills over 100+ closes, at least +2 cents a contract better after fee than the live 1-second rule, both halves positive; about 7-10 days. Any live change goes to you first.

**B. About 1 pin order in 5 is priced on the previous second's index print.**
- In the pin bot's live logs 10-10..10-16, 170 of 339 orders were decided in the first 200 ms of a clock second; an even spread would put about 68 there.
- For 10-10..10-14 (238 orders, before the clock drift below): the 50 decided at 0-99 ms priced on a print with a median age of 1.02 s; the 90 decided at 100-199 ms on a print 0.13 s old.
- Likely cause, NOT verified in the code: the scan's entry gates open when the clock second ticks over, about 90 ms before that second's print arrives.
- Next test: log, for every order, when the print it priced on arrived, next to the decision time; then check on our own live fills whether stale-print orders lose more often. (This is not the dead "no sends in the first 100 ms" rule, SH158, which looked at send time and money, not the print's age.)

**C. Operations.**
- The laptop clock drifted up to about 0.6 s: +467 to +637 ms between 5 AM and noon ET on 10-16, during the Wi-Fi outage; 0.02 s at 10:17 PM ET after a resync. The pin's 2-second index-age gate and the forecaster's late/stale limits compare laptop time with exchange time, so they inherit it. Money impact not measured.
- The recorder's Kalshi connection stalls about 1 second in 5: index prints and book updates arrive 0.3-2 s late together, while the Bitstamp feed on the same laptop and the forecaster's own connection do not. Our recorded fills are unaffected (the book is rebuilt on Kalshi's own timestamps), but any study that uses the recorder's receive time as "when we knew" gets a fake 1-second tail.
