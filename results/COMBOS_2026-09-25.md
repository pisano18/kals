# Combos on our 15-minute crypto markets: would they make more?

Written 2026-09-25 about 12:15 PM ET. Nothing was bought, asked for or created: every call was a read.
Sources, best first: our own orders and Kalshi's settlement ledger, Kalshi's public trade feed (other
people's combo trades), and our recorder's leg prices at the same second. No replay was used.

## Answer: no

**At the moments our bot actually buys, a combo would have charged us about the same for our pick, or a
little more.** Within 2 seconds of 26 of our real orders (23 closes, 09-22 to 09-25), combos containing
our exact pick priced it between 0.0c and 0.4c per contract above what we paid (middle case +0.4c to
+0.7c; dearer in 15-17 of the 26). For combo trades of 10+ contracts, closer to our size, it was 0.5c to
0.9c dearer (16 closes). No discount showed up.

**Why:** whoever sells combos prices them near the real odds. Our money comes from leg offers that
are priced *below* the real odds. The combo seller doesn't pass that bargain on.

**At the same size a combo is the same bet as our two picks.** It ties up half the cash, but a losing
close costs the same. So it can't earn more unless it is priced lower, and it isn't.

## How combos work (for a small API account like ours)

- A combo is one contract that pays $1 only if **every** leg wins. Kalshi lists all 7 of our coins'
  15-minute markets (for the next 4 closes) as allowed legs, plus NEAR, ZEC and the hourly ladders.
  Rules: at least 2 legs, one market per coin per close.
- To buy one you: (1) ask Kalshi to create the market for that exact set of legs (a write; limit
  5,000 a week); (2) send a request for a price (a write); (3) sellers answer privately; (4) you accept;
  (5) the seller has 3 seconds to confirm, then a 1-second wait, then it fills. Our own code can't do
  this today. `ordercli.py` only sends ordinary orders.
- **Speed:** fills came a median 0.6 seconds after the request (73 fills on 17 combos). In the last
  45 seconds before a close it was a median 1.8 seconds, and 1 fill in 10 took about 7 seconds or more (13
  fills). Our leg orders take about 0.12 seconds.
- **Late trading works.** Combos fill right up to the close (fills seen 1-5 seconds before it). Of
  209,032 traded combos with a 15-minute leg (09-16 8 PM ET to 09-25 11 AM ET), 1,266 were created in
  the last 45 seconds and 21 in the last 10.
- **Who sells them:** we can't see. Kalshi's list of active sellers is empty for all 2,446 events in
  all 3 open collections, NFL included, so that list tells us nothing. The trades show that someone
  does sell them, and a lot: about 27,000 crypto/commodity 15-minute combos trade a day (5-6 million
  contracts, roughly $1 million a day).
- **Size:** most buys are about $10. The biggest near-certain buy was 4,324 contracts ($4,292), 4
  seconds before a close. Our 88 contracts (about $83) is a normal size.
- **Fee:** the buyer pays the usual 7% x price x (1 - price), on the combo price. For two 97c picks
  that's 0.39c per contract versus 0.41c for buying both legs. Almost the same.
- **Payout:** combos paid out about 8 minutes after the close (median 469-478 seconds, measured at the
  10:45 and 11:00 AM ET closes today). The legs pay out in about 20 seconds.
- **No undo:** you can't cancel a filled combo. Selling one back means asking for another price, and
  sellers paid about 4c below the legs' value for near-certain combos.

## What combos actually traded at (other people's trades)

For each combo trade, I compared the combo's price with the legs bought one by one at the same second.
The sample covers 60 closes from 09-17 8 PM ET to 09-25 11 AM ET.

| Near-certain combos bought (legs worth 90c+ together) | trades | closes | combo minus the legs' asking prices |
|---|---|---|---|
| all | 375 | 51 | 0.4c cheaper (give or take 0.2c) |
| last 10 s before the close | 60 | 30 | 1.3c cheaper |
| ... but at least one leg had **no seller at all** (ask shown as $1) | 302 of 375 | 50 | 0.3c cheaper |
| **like ours: 2-3 legs, every leg offered at 90-99c** | **16** | **14** | **0.4c cheaper, give or take 1.0c: no real difference** |

The "cheaper" rows look like a discount, but they aren't one we can collect. Most of them are measured
against an asking price nobody was actually offering. At random moments the legs' asks sit *above* the
real odds, so a combo priced near the real odds looks cheap. At our moments, when the leg has a bargain
offer, the combo is dearer (the answer above).

## The money, side by side

Two 97c picks, 88 contracts each, before fees:

| | Legs (today) | One combo of 88 at 94.09c |
|---|---|---|
| cash tied up | $170.72 | $82.80 |
| both win | +$5.28 | +$5.20 |
| one loses | -$82.72 | -$82.80 |
| both lose | -$170.72 | -$82.80 |

- **Loss count and the brakes don't change at the same size.** A close with one losing pick costs
  about $83 either way. The combo only saves money when both picks lose. **In our record that has
  never happened: 0 of 220 closes where we held 2+ picks.**
- **The trap:** "half the cash" tempts buying twice as many. That doubles the bet. A losing close
  would cost about $166, and the $200 day loss cap would trip after 2 losing closes instead of 3.
- **Our own record (live orders + Kalshi ledger, 09-08 to 09-25 10:45 AM ET):** 916 markets; the pick
  lost in 21 (about 2 in 100). 204 closes had 2+ picks at 90c+. 4 of them had one losing pick; none had
  two. Buying those same closes as combos at equal size:

| combo priced vs what we paid for the legs | 17 days, 204 closes | per day |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5c cheaper (best case, not what we measured) | +$30.29 | +$1.75 |
| the same | -$7.67 | -$0.44 |
| 0.4-0.5c dearer (what we measured at our moments) | -$38 to -$46 | -$2.20 to -$2.64 |

- **Timing makes it worse.** A combo needs every pick bought at the same moment. Our two picks in a
  close came a median 11 seconds apart. Only 3 of 39 were within 2 seconds, and 9 of 39 within 5.

## Verdict and smallest test

Don't build it. The best case is under $2 a day. What we measured is a small loss, and it needs a new
way of placing orders that fills 5-15x slower than ours.

If you want the price checked directly anyway, the smallest safe test costs nothing and risks nothing.
On about 20 closes where the bot buys 2 picks, create the combo and ask for a price for the same
count. Log the prices offered next to what the bot paid, and **never accept**. It's still a write to
Kalshi (it creates a market and a price request), so it needs your OK and new code. Only if the offered
prices come in at least 0.5c under what the bot pays would a 1-contract live buy be worth trying.

## How it was measured (for the record)

- Rules: GET only. Nothing was created, asked for, ordered or cancelled. The recorder's tape was
  only read, one hour file at a time.
- Combo lists: `GET /markets?series_ticker=KXMVECROSSCATEGORY&min_close_ts&max_close_ts` for every
  quarter hour from 09-17 00:00Z to 09-25 15:00Z (783 closes with traded combos). Trades:
  `GET /markets/trades` for the last 60-95 s before 60 sampled closes (1,857 combo trades on
  15-minute-only combos), plus +-12 s around each of our 197 orders that have millisecond times.
  Leg prices: the recorder's `ticker` channel, the last bid/ask at or before each trade. Request to
  fill: `GET /communications/rfqs?market_ticker=` for 17 combos.
- The "at our moments" figure values the other legs in each combo at their mid (+0.4c) and at their
  asking price (+0.0c). Both are shown because the answer depends slightly on which you use. Both are
  within 2 s of our order, one average per order, and no seconds-wide window showed a real discount.
  Wider windows (5 s) average 0.3-0.7c cheaper, but only because a pick's price can move several cents
  in a few seconds near the close. Their middle case is still +0.1c to +0.4c dearer.
- The settlement ledger was written 09-25 14:45Z. Scripts are in the session scratchpad (`c5`-`c19`),
  not the repo.
- Kalshi docs used: RFQ guide (docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rfqs), multivariate collection and
  create-market endpoints, the changelog (combo tick sizes 09-10, combo maker fees 08-20/08-22), and
  the Kalshi help-center pages on Combos and Fees. `GET /series/KXMVECROSSCATEGORY` returned
  `fee_type: quadratic_with_combo_maker_fees`, multiplier 1.
