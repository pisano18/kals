### 1. Hourly temperature "pin" (KXTEMPMIAH, KXTEMPNYCH, LAX, CHI) -- PROMISING

**What it is.** Kalshi's hourly temperature markets settle on Kalshi's OWN
minute-by-minute city temperature index, which is public and needs no login
(`GET https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/live_data/weather/{city}`).
The newest published reading is 5-6 minutes old. The index almost never
moves in those last 6 minutes (Miami: half the time it moves 0.00 F, 9 in
10 times under 0.36 F, worst 1.08 F over 165 closes). So the settlement
number is effectively known 6 minutes before the market closes -- and
people are still buying the losing side at 50-97c in those minutes. Same
family as pin (a public near-certain settlement the market is slow to
price), with a 5-6 minute window instead of 45 seconds.

**Why the earlier lens killed it and why that was wrong.** The lens compared
settlement to the airport weather-station reading and got 82% agreement
(bad). The verifier found the real source: the settlement equalled the
Kalshi index at the close minute on 165 of 165 Miami closes and 164 of 165
NYC closes.

**Money on the tape (somebody bought it, not our result).** Buying the
index's side at 50-97c in the last 6 minutes when the index was at least
1 F clear of the strike: Miami +$1,248 over 89 closes in 7 days (about
$175/day, positive every day: +125, +184, +148, +194, +221, +200, +161,
+15), NYC +$145 over 41 closes. At a 1 F margin the index side was right
1,328 of 1,328 times (Miami) and 1,312 of 1,312 (NYC). Tape ceiling ~$200/day
versus pin's $27-45/day.

**The danger.** The tail. At only a 0.5 F margin Miami lost 2-3 of ~1,489
markets and one such loss at 95c costs $900-2,500 on the tape in a day. If
the index is 10 minutes stale instead of 6, even a 1 F margin took one
loss (-$2,486 on 09-22). The margin must be sized to the 6-minute move
distribution per city. Also: our fills will be the picked-over subset
(same as pin), book DEPTH at 90-97c is unmeasured (weather is not on our
WebSocket tape), and money sits 25-35 minutes after close before settling.

**Expected dollars.** Unknown live; tape ceiling ~$200/day across Miami +
NYC; realistic guess $20-80/day if depth is a fraction of the tape.
Confidence 0.35 (verifier's mechanism confirmation, not the lens's 0.03).

**Test to run next (read-only, 3-5 days, `scratchpad/vt/vt_wxshadow.py`).**
1. Every 60 s pull the public index for miami/nyc/lax/chicago; log the age
   of the newest point. Table: age p50/p90/max per city, minutes with age
   over 7 minutes.
2. For every hourly market, from 8 minutes before close to close, pull the
   order book every 15 s for strikes within 3 F of the index; log asks and
   sizes on the index side.
3. From the archived index, pick the per-city margin at which the index
   NEVER crossed the strike in the last 6 minutes.
4. **Pass bar, written before looking:** index age p90 under 7 minutes;
   offered (not traded) index-side contracts at <=97c with margin >=1 F at
   least 50 per day per city; zero index crossings of a >=1 F margin in the
   shadow period. Fail any one and stop.
5. Then a paper arm on the same code path, then a 1-contract live arm with
   per-order sign-off, loss rate quoted from live fills only.

**Needs.** No key, no new feed. A new small poller process. A taker path via
`ordercli.py` / `pintake` with its own caller window. A cap on concurrent
exposure because settlement is 25-35 minutes after close.

### 2. Hourly BTC strike ladder (KXBTCD) -- TESTABLE-NOW

**What it is.** The hourly BTC ladder settles on the IDENTICAL rule as our
15-minute markets (60 seconds of CF Benchmarks BRTI averaged), on the same
index feed we already hold. Every hour has ~188 strikes $100 apart, so
there is always a strike sitting $5-60 from the settling average whose
holders bail in the last minute. Same fair-value math, same bot; only the
market list changes.

**Why the 09-15 kill was wrong.** It sampled ONE close's book (188 strikes
x 42 samples) and found 1 buyable of 2,568. On 7 days of trades, cheap
near-certain prints appear on 47 of 164 hourly closes (29%), so a one-close
sample finds nothing 71% of the time by chance. Underpowered, not
contradicted.

**Money on the tape (somebody bought it).** Last 3-30 s: 144,873 contracts
on 37 closes at average 96.1c, +$5,214, 3 contracts lost. 31-45 s: +$3,359,
0 lost. Beyond 60 s: LOSES 8.2% of contracts (-$55,836) -- the 60-s wall,
confirmed on a second product. Capped at the live bot's budget (2 markets x
71 contracts per close): +$98 per 7 days = **$14/day**; at 142/market
$28/day. Depth is the prize: median 1,899 cheap contracts per close, 27x the
15M's median touch of 69. It is incremental: on 21 of the 47 cheap hourly
closes we had no 15M fill at all.

**The danger.** The SAME capped-tape metric on our KXBTC15M reads about
-$5/day this week while our real BTC15M ledger since 09-17 is 63 markets,
4,986 contracts, **-$469.50**. So the tape overstates our result on the
product we already trade; $14/day is a ceiling and live capture could be
~$0. Cheap prints are one-second sweeps by fast takers (median 73% of a
close's cheap contracts land in one second). The hourly settles on the same
second as all twelve 15M closes: doubling BTC exposure on one outcome.

**Expected dollars.** $0-14/day at today's size. Confidence 0.35.

**Test to run next (read-only paper arm, 7 days).** `pinrun --paper
--series KXBTCD` with a ladder-aware universe: page the whole event, keep
the 3 strikes nearest the live BRTI (pinrun's refresh pulls only 4 markets
per series today, `pinrun.py:14766`, and would miss the near-money rung);
strike from `custom_strike.floor_strike` as today; count against the same
per-close budget as the coincident 15M closes. Log per close: fired /
no_offer / refused, best ask at decision, depth at <=98c, paper fill.
**Pass bar, written now:** >=30 fired closes; 0 paper losses at <=30 s;
paper-captured contracts >=20% of the tape's capped figure (>=660 of 3,305
for the week); offers visible at <=98c on >=15 of the ~47 cheap closes.
Then a 1-contract penny test with per-order sign-off, 30 closes. Add
KXBTCD books to the collector only after the SSD (disk is the constraint).

### 3. Two-sided 1-contract quotes at the touch, 10-90c, on the up/down series -- TESTABLE-NOW

**What it is.** Rest a 1-contract buy and sell at the current best price on
both sides of every up/down market, only between 10c and 90c, re-post at
the back of the queue whenever the price moves, hold whatever fills to
settlement. Makers pay no fee. `research/informed.py` showed on 09-06 that
people who trade AT the touch carry no information (a resting quote there
nets +0.48c per filled contract, t=6.4, 17.1M fills). The missing number
was always how many fills a small resting order gets from behind the queue.
`research/queuesim.py` was written to answer that on 2026-09-06 and NEVER
RUN until tonight.

**What the simulator says (replay, oldest tape 08-25..09-03, 9 days, 829
closes).** 1 contract per side: positive on 9 of 9 days, $162-$552/day,
median $317/day, +0.81c per filled contract, t=6.8, ~44,000 fills/day.
Median close is positive (+$4.26 and +$1.55 on the two days checked; 60/96
and 49/81 closes positive) so the heavy tail sits on a positive centre.
Drop-the-best-10-closes still positive 7 of 9 days. 10 contracts per side:
median $3,165/day but worst single close -$372. Kalshi's write limit is 100
orders/s (docs fetched), so 24 order ops/s at full breadth is not binding.

**The danger.** THE BACKTEST IS NOT EVIDENCE (standing rule). It is the
oldest tape, before the mid-September supply change; the sim's 1-second
clock lets it join every fresh touch even though the touch survives to the
next second only 39-45% of the time, so fills are an upper bound and our
latency is unmodelled; no price band clears its own detection limit; and at
10 contracts the daily swing (-$877 to +$4,413 on one day's band) is
ruinous for a $900 bank. The only admissible number is a live one.

**Expected dollars.** Sim says $160-550/day at 1 contract per side; live
unknown. Confidence 0.25.

**Test to run next.** Stage A (sim, no money, ~2.5 h, needs >2 GB RAM free,
run when the paper fleet is small): rebuild `flow_cache` for 09-18..09-23
(`python research/flow.py --data C:\kals\kalshi_data --out C:\kals\fulltape`),
run `scratchpad/v2mk/v2_qs_days.py <day>`, `v2_zone.py`, `v2_dist.py`.
**Bar:** per-fill >=+0.3c with t>=2 pooled over the 5 recent days;
drop-top-10 positive on >=3 of 5; fills/day within 2x of the old tape's
44,044. Stage B (live, operator sign-off, one day then five): 1-contract
post-only quoter via `ordercli.py` on KXSOL15M + KXXRP15M, 10-90c, net
inventory cap 10 per market, $30 day-loss cap, `self_trade_prevention
'maker'` so the pin bot can never lift our own offer. **Bars:** live fills
>=25% of the sim's for the same day/series; per-fill >=0c over >=60 closes;
no close worse than -$15. **Kill:** live fills <10% of sim, or per-fill
<=-0.5c over 60 closes. Only then 10 contracts, then more series.

### 4. Biggest-quake-of-the-day watch (KXBIGGESTQUAKE) -- TESTABLE-NOW, small

**What it is.** Once USGS posts a magnitude at or above a strike, that
strike's YES is locked. 22 locked markets on 6 quake-days, 0 mismatches
against USGS revised magnitudes; the pre-quake YES price was 22-90c so the
jump is real. Tape money after origin +10 min: $721 at <=97c over 6
quake-days (min $45, median $128, max $240 per quake-day). Kalshi
early-closes the market 11-602 minutes after the quake.

**The danger.** $706 of the $1,486 total cheap money lands in the first 10
minutes after the quake, before USGS normally posts (the crowd trades on
shaking reports). We only have REVISED magnitudes; an initial 5.4 that
revises to 5.3 is a false lock. Whole series is 47k contracts a week with
an 8c spread; 6 clusters.

**Expected dollars.** $0-10/day. Confidence 0.05.

**Test to run next (read-only, 14 days, `scratchpad/ok/quake_watch.py`).**
Poll USGS GeoJSON every 30 s for M>=5.0, record first-seen magnitude and
re-read for 24 h to log revisions; pull open KXBIGGESTQUAKE markets every
5 min; when first-seen magnitude >= strike, snapshot the order book every
5 s for 30 min. **Bar:** >=$10/day of resting <=97c depth visible >=30 s
after USGS first post on >= half the quake-days, AND zero false locks.
Otherwise drop.

### 5. Rest a bid for the Coin Race leader at 90-99c -- REFINED FRAGMENT, tiny

The market is pricing boundary risk, not giving it away. (Lens also used the wrong climate day: it is local STANDARD time, 01:00-01:00 during daylight saving.)

3. **AAA gas price ladders** -- every strike the market priced at >=80c settled the market's way (217 of 217 over 12 days); the certainty comes from a private station-price feed we don't have; takers lost about -$9,800 on the tape across all bands and one jump day wiped 20 days of wins.
4. **Equity-index and hourly-commodity families (KXINXU, KXNASDAQ100U, KXDJI, KXGOLDH/WTIH/SILVERH, KXEURUSD15M, KXUSDJPY15M)** -- all settle on ONE value at ONE instant, so there is no partly-written average to read (THEORY 1b); the same class lost real money on 09-17/18 (-$53.06, -$27.28); spreads 4-23c, volume 3-5 orders of magnitude below BTC15M.
5. **KXLOWT* daily lows** -- the free feed is whole-Celsius (1.8 F steps) and the settlement source read the day's low 1.6 F ABOVE the observation on 5 of 33 locks: false locks. At a safe 1.8 F margin the money is $5.63 over 12 city-days.
6. **KXRAIN** -- all the winner money is gone within 15 minutes of the first rain observation and YES was already 95-99c the hour before (radar).
7. **Gemini Predictions pin** -- on 6 of 6 closes recorded at 1-second cadence (9 of 9 lifetime) the winning side was NEVER buyable at <=98c inside the last 45 s; the only resting orders are 5-contract asks on the LOSING side. Priced against our 27 real hedges: perp fees $6,254 vs binary hedge fees $13.92, 449x. Fourth kill of this one.

Already measured 09-06 (CROSS_VENUE.md); the false premise came from `C:\kals\poly_us.py`'s header. 11. **Deribit / OKX / Bybit / Kraken / Coinbase derivatives / Crypto.com** -- no US-accessible venue lists a sub-hour crypto binary on a 60-s average; Crypto.com's book dies at 42 s with 13-24c spreads; the only other use (a hedge) is item 8.

15. **Resting the hedge as a maker bid** (either the unfilled remainder after an alarm, or a hedge bid at entry) -- a bid that rests can only be filled when OUR side is getting MORE certain, never during the collapse it insures: 0 of 75 shortfall contracts recoverable at the first ask; the at-entry form fills 96% of winners and pays for itself on 40% of losers, -$1,136 over 16 days on our own 800 markets.
