## 0. PRE-REGISTERED BAR -- written before the week's money was computed

Written 2026-09-25 02:25 UTC at git `8620f06` (working tree, makersim.py uncommitted), after the
self-test passed and after ONE smoke hour (20260920T14) had been looked at
for plumbing (anomaly counters, RSS, inventory bounds) -- that hour's P&L was
seen and is disclosed in section 6; the bar below is set for the OTHER 131
hours, and the smoke hour is reported separately so nobody can say the bar
was fitted to it.

The decision this bar governs: **is a 1-contract live post-only test worth
building?** "Worth building" means ALL of:

- **B1 (the conservative model earns).** Under `last` (we are always behind
  everything displayed; only a taker who sweeps THROUGH the level, or an order
  that would have crossed us, fills us) at 300 ms reaction latency: realised
  P&L per filled contract >= +0.30c with clustered-by-close t >= 2.0 over
  >= 200 closes, AND positive on at least 4 of the 6 days.
- **B2 (enough fills to learn from live).** The same variant gives >= 200
  fills/day across the four series (so a 5-day live test would clear 60
  closes with fills).
- **B3 (a bad day is survivable at 1 contract).** No day worse than -$15 and
  no single close worse than -$10 under `last` L=300.
- **B4 (latency does not flip the sign).** `fifo_prorata` at L=300 is also
  >= 0c per contract (t not required) -- i.e. the pick-off during the
  cancel/re-post round trip does not eat the whole edge.

If any of B1-B4 fails, the answer is "not worth building at this design", and
the report says which bar failed and by how much. If all pass, Stage B (live,
per-order sign-off) inherits the bars already written in IDEAS_2026-09-24.md
item 3: live fills >= 25% of this sim's `last` L=300 count for the same
days/series; per-fill >= 0c over >= 60 closes with fills; no close worse than
-$15; kill at per-fill <= -0.5c over 60 closes or live fills < 10% of sim.

MDE is stated before every estimate below. n is closes (all four series
settle on the same quarter hour, so a close is one cluster).

## 8. How the queue was modelled, and what the tape cannot tell us

**What is on the tape and was used.** Every order-book change (`orderbook_delta`,
~3.8M lines an hour, fractional contracts), the opening book of each market
(`orderbook_snapshot`), every trade print (`trade`, one print per resting order
hit, with the taker's side), and the exchange's own settlement result
(`market_lifecycle_v2` "determined"). A print and the delta that removes the
size it consumed carry the same exchange millisecond (21,545 of 21,545 checked),
so consumption is netted against the next negative delta at that level and the
remainder is a cancel. Nothing was merged on the collector's receive clock: the
trade channel arrives 4-6 s late, the delta channel ~26 ms late.

**Our order.** 1.00 contract at the touch on each side of each market while
that side's touch is between 10c and 90c. Decisions are taken at the end of
each exchange millisecond (a sweep is atomic). When the touch moves we cancel
and re-post at the new touch, at the BACK of the queue: `ahead` = everything
displayed there when our order lands. Fills are held to settlement. Net
position capped at 10 a market, counting orders still in flight.

**How we get filled (each rule is checked by the self-test against a
hand-computed world).**
1. `queue`: a print at our price larger than what was in front of us.
2. `walk`: a taker cleared our level and printed at the next one -- they would
   have taken our contract first. Credited only when our level showed nothing
   in front of us (or emptied within the same millisecond).
3. `cross`: an order arrived on the other side at a price that would have
   matched us before it could rest.
4. A taker who cleared the level exactly and stopped is assumed to have wanted
   exactly that -- no fill for us (conservative; assumption A4).

**Queue models.** `last`: everything displayed is always in front of us
(arrivals after us included) -- the requested conservative bound; it can only
fill by `walk` or `cross`. `fifo_behind` / `fifo_prorata` / `fifo_front`: true
price-time priority, differing only in whether a cancel is assumed to come
from behind us, pro rata, or from in front of us. The tape shows a level
shrinking; it never says whose order left.

**Latency.** L = 0 / 300 / 1000 ms from an exchange event to our order landing
or dying. A cancel needs the order id, which arrives one round trip after
posting, so an order lives at least L ms. During that window our old order is
still live at its old price with its old queue position, and gets filled there
-- the `stale` fills. L = 0 is a bound nobody can reach; 300 ms is a guess for
a bot on this laptop over REST; 1000 ms is a bad day.

**What the tape cannot verify (stated, not assumed away).**
- A1. Our contract does not change anyone else's behaviour or move the touch.
  At 1 contract against a median of ~60 in front of us that is close to true;
  it is not true at 10 or 100.
- A2. The latencies are guesses; Kalshi's matching delay and our REST round
  trip are not on the tape. The dependence on L in section 1 is the answer to
  "how much does it matter": a lot.
- A3. Self-trade: the pin bot buys the near-certain side at 90-98c and our
  asks stop at 90c, so it can only lift our ask when its belief is >99% at a
  yes price at or under 90c. Live this needs `self_trade_prevention` on both
  bots; the sim assumes it never happens.
- A4. A taker who cleared the level exactly and did not walk or rest a
  remainder is assumed to have wanted exactly that size (no fill).
- A5. The collector subscribes ~2 minutes after each market opens; those
  minutes are not simulated.
- A6. Fractional prints are taken at face value; our order is 1.00.
- A7. Every market whose settlement message is missing from the tape was
  excluded (count in the run line above), not guessed.


## 9. VERDICT against section 0 -- written 2026-09-25 ~05:5xZ

**Not worth building at this design. B1, B3 and B4 fail by wide margins; B2 passes.**

**Coverage, stated first.** The run covered **61 hours (2026-09-18 00Z to
2026-09-20 12Z), 968 markets, 413 closes, 3 days** -- not the 6 days the
bar names. It stopped itself at 2026-09-25 03:26Z (its own memory guard wrote
`mm_hour_20260920T12_abort.json` in the minute free RAM fell to ~1.06 GB on
the box). Progress figures quoted during the run ("80 / 126 of 132 hours")
were counts of FILES, two per hour; they were wrong by 2x. The title line of
the tables above says "126 of 132 hours" for the same reason; the run line
under it (61 hour files) is the true coverage. The bar's "4 of 6 days" is
therefore read as "every day it covered"; no plausible 3 further days can
reverse a t of -51.

| bar | needed | measured (`last`, L = 300 ms unless stated) | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 per contract | >= +0.30c, t >= 2.0, >= 200 closes, most days + | **-1.60c** close-weighted, t = -51.7, 413 closes, 0 of 3 days positive | **FAIL** by 1.9c |
| B2 fills | >= 200 fills/day | 183,937 fills/day | pass |
| B3 bad day | no day < -$15, no close < -$10 | worst day **-$3,409**, worst close **-$100** | **FAIL** |
| B4 latency | `fifo_prorata` L=300 >= 0c | **-1.18c** | **FAIL** |

**Even the best case loses.** The most generous model in the table (we sit
at the FRONT of every queue and react in zero time) still loses 0.15c a
contract, on every day. Hand check: -1.60c x 176,458 contracts/day =
-$2,823/day against the -$2,771 printed (the printed figure is pooled; the
close-weighted per-contract figure differs by 0.03c).

**Why (section 4).** At 300 ms reaction time 52-78% of our fills happen
while we are trying to pull a quote the price has already moved through --
the "stale" column, -2.0c a contract, -$8,000 over three days. The side we
SELL loses in every variant (ask $ column, -$8,400 to -$10,900); fills get
worse toward the close (-3c to -8c a contract inside 60 s). That is the
informed-flow picture the toxicity work found from the taker side, seen now
from the maker side: whoever trades against a resting quote in these
markets usually knows where the index is going.

**Plain-language conclusion for the operator.** Posting our own offers and
waiting to be filled -- the "$160-550 a day" idea from the idea hunt -- loses
money in every version we could simulate, on every day, even when we assume
we are faster and first in line. It is dead on these markets. The one
maker idea left is Polymarket US, where makers are PAID a rebate
(SECOND_INCOME_SCAN_2026-09-25 section 2); this simulation says nothing
about that book and is not evidence for or against it.

**Caveat on the source.** This is a replay of the recorded order books,
which our standing rule says is not evidence of a profit. It IS allowed to
kill: every assumption in it (queue position, latency, cancel order) was
set in our favour in at least one variant, and all of them lose.
