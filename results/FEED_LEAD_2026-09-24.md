since 09-13. Q1 and Q2 are measured on 4 days (2026-09-21 00:00 UTC ..

## The answer, in one paragraph

The exchange books we record DO run ahead of what the bot holds: they
explain 61-78% of the next index print (sign right 74-90 times out of 100
on the best feed per coin, 4 days), and the bot only has a print 0.2 s
(median) to 1.0 s (slowest tenth) after its timestamp. **But that
head start would not have saved money.** Before 6 of our 8 losers in the
coins we have a feed for, the books had not moved against us at all in the
last 0.9 s (median move 0.00 bps, same as the winners). The losses happened
AFTER we entered; a feed cannot warn about a move that has not happened.
The best of 15 rule cells would have refused 9 entries for +$78 net over
12 days (2 losers avoided, 7 winners forgone), p = 0.011 against a bar of
0.0033 for 15 looks -- not evidence. The BTC-index flag the verifier
already wrote a PREREG for, which needs no new feed, is stronger on the
same fills (4 of 19 flagged entries lost, p = 0.0005). **Do not build the
constituent feed into the bot as a loss gate.**

hedges are a separate kind and excluded), first entry per market, 651
markets, all 651 settled in results/kalshi_ledger.json, 0 dropped. Loser =

## Q1 -- does the constituent book lead the print? (4 days; exchange clocks)

MDE: with ~282,000 seconds per coin-feed a correlation of 0.01 is
detectable; the limit is the 100-ms scan step and the feeds' update
cadence, not n. (The one-hour pilot on 09-23 10-11 ET gave R^2 0.05-0.10
higher across the board; the 4-day numbers below are the ones to quote.)

Method: for every consecutive pair of index prints, the index's 1-s change
is compared with the constituent mid's 1-s change shifted by k ms
(k = -1000..+1000). The peak k says which moment of the book the print
reflects; k < 0 means the print reflects OLDER books. Mids are interpolated
between quotes so a sparse feed does not fake a lag (self-test: planted lags
of 0/300/600 ms recovered within 100 ms, dense and sparse; a null world
stays flat).

| coin | feed | n seconds | print reflects the book of ... ms earlier (peak k) | R^2 at peak | R^2 of the plain previous second (what a live rule would use) | sign right (previous second) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BTC | Coinbase | 281,838 | 100 | 0.70 | 0.69 | 76% |
| BTC | Bitstamp | 282,092 | 0 | 0.62 | 0.62 | 80% |
| ETH | Coinbase | 281,807 | 100 | 0.61 | 0.55 | 69% |
| ETH | Bitstamp | 282,086 | 0 | 0.61 | 0.61 | 75% |
| SOL | Bitstamp | 282,081 | 0 | 0.65 | 0.65 | 90% |
| SOL | Coinbase | 281,813 | 100 | 0.59 | 0.55 | 84% |
| XRP | Bitstamp | 282,084 | 100 | 0.66 | 0.65 | 79% |
| XRP | Coinbase | 281,817 | 200 | 0.62 | 0.53 | 73% |
| DOGE | Bitstamp | 282,075 | 0 | 0.78 | 0.78 | 80% |
| DOGE | Coinbase | 281,667 | 100 | 0.56 | 0.52 | 65% |
| ADA | Bitstamp | 282,077 | 200 | 0.74 | 0.65 | 74% |
| BCH | Bitstamp | 282,077 | 200 | 0.70 | 0.62 | 72% |
| BTC | Kraken / Gemini | 282,053 / 281,991 | +200 / +100 (trade-stamped, sparse; not usable for timing) | 0.35 / 0.49 | 0.30 / 0.48 | 60% / 73% |

Index 1-s move size over the 4 days (sd of the 1-s change, median hour):
BTC 0.57 bps, ETH 0.70, SOL 0.94, XRP 1.10, ADA 1.43, DOGE 1.53, BCH 1.75.

The print is contemporaneous with the books or at most 200 ms behind them
(BTC/ETH/SOL/DOGE on Bitstamp: 0 ms; ADA/BCH: 200 ms), and the books
explain 61-78% of it on the best feed. The usable head start is therefore
NOT a lag inside CF's calculation; it is the 0.2-1.0 s between the print's
timestamp and the bot holding it, plus up to 1 s between prints. Joint fit
of the six sub-windows: for BTC on Bitstamp the last 200 ms before the
timestamp is fully in the print (coefficient ~1); for the alts it is only
partly in (0.04-0.7) and the 200-500 ms window is (0.6-1.0); nothing after
the timestamp is (0.0-0.2), so CF's clock and the exchanges' agree.

Reverse check (alt now vs BTC next second): corr 0.02-0.05 for every alt,
so the direction is BTC -> alt. Two seconds later it is gone. The SAME
second is the strong one (after a big BTC second the alt's print moves the
same way 75-99 times out of 100); the NEXT second is weak (53-66 after a
big BTC second, 52-56 overall). BTC's Bitstamp book is BTC's print a
second early, so a BTC feed would turn the strong same-second relation
into a usable one-second warning for the alts -- that is the only place a
constituent feed adds to the verifier's existing BTC-index flag (Q3's
"incl. entry second" row: 27 flagged, 4 losers, vs 19 / 4 without it).
Marginal on our fills so far.

## Q4 -- could the bot use this live? (if a use ever justifies it)

- The bot already runs its index socket as a daemon thread (`IndexWS._run`,
  websockets + asyncio). A `ConstituentWS` of the same shape on Bitstamp's
  public `order_book_<coin>usd` channel (no key, 100-ms snapshots, all feed
  coins on one socket) is ~150 lines plus tests; it keeps per coin a ring of
  (local ms, mid) and exposes `move_against(coin, side, window_ms)`.
- Cost: crypto_feeds.py does four exchanges in 36 MB at ~135 msg/s; one
  Bitstamp socket is ~10 msg/s per coin, small next to the 20 Hz loop.
  Latency to us 72-85 ms.
- Where it would sit: entry path only, next to `fresh_level_block`
  (pinrun.py ~line 16160), flag ships OFF, self-test that it cannot touch
  the hedge pass, PREREG on live fills before it is turned on.
- If the feed dies: FAIL OPEN -- no quote in the last 2 s means "no
  opinion", never "refuse"; the bot trades exactly as today.
- About one day of work including the startup-path test with the flag
  applied. **Not justified by anything measured here.**
