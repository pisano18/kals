## Q1 -- does the constituent book lead the print? (one hour; exchange clocks)

MDE: with 3,209 seconds per coin a correlation of 0.05 is detectable, so
everything in the table is far above noise; the limit is the 100-ms scan
step and the feeds' own update cadence, not sample size.

Method: for every consecutive pair of index prints, the index's 1-second
change is compared with the constituent mid's 1-second change shifted by
k ms (k = -1000..+1000). The peak k says which moment of the book the print
reflects: k < 0 means the print reflects OLDER books, i.e. the book led.
Mids are interpolated between quotes so a sparse feed does not fake a lag
(self-tested: planted lags of 0/300/600 ms recovered within 100 ms, sparse
and dense; a null world stays flat).

| coin | best feed | print reflects the book of ... ms earlier (peak k) | R^2 at peak | sign right | R^2 of the plain previous second (what a live rule would use) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BTC | Coinbase | 100 | 0.79 | 82% | 0.75 |
| BTC | Bitstamp | 0 | 0.72 | 84% | 0.72 |
| ETH | Coinbase | 100 | 0.71 | 79% | 0.61 |
| ETH | Bitstamp | 100 | 0.65 | 76% | 0.65 |
| SOL | Bitstamp | 100 | 0.76 | 90% | 0.74 |
| SOL | Coinbase | 200 | 0.72 | 88% | 0.60 |
| XRP | Coinbase | 300 | 0.59 | 79% | 0.46 |
| XRP | Bitstamp | 200 | 0.55 | 80% | 0.50 |
| DOGE | Bitstamp | 0 | 0.75 | 85% | 0.75 |
| DOGE | Coinbase | 100 | 0.72 | 76% | 0.71 |
| ADA | Bitstamp | 200 | 0.74 | 83% | 0.61 |
| BCH | Coinbase | 200 | 0.75 | 74% | 0.61 |
| BTC | Kraken / Gemini | +200 / +100 (their quotes are trade-stamped and sparse; not usable for timing) | 0.48 / 0.42 | 71% / 76% | 0.41 / 0.39 |

Reading: the print is close to contemporaneous with the books (0-300 ms
behind them, XRP the slowest), and the books explain ~three quarters of it.
The lead that matters for money is NOT this 0-300 ms; it is the 200-1,000 ms
between the print's timestamp and the moment the bot holds it, plus the up
to 1 s between prints. In that gap the feed already shows where the next
print is going, ~75% of the time in size and ~85% of the time in sign.

Which part of the second the print already contains (joint fit, all six
sub-windows; coefficient ~1 = fully in the print, ~0 = not yet): for BTC on
Bitstamp the last 200 ms before the print's timestamp is fully in it (1.01);
for the alts on Coinbase/Bitstamp the last 200 ms is only partly in it
(0.04-0.69) and the 200-500 ms window is (0.6-1.0). Nothing after the
timestamp is in it (0.0-0.2), so the exchange clocks and CF's clock agree.

## Q2 -- does BTC's index move predict the alts' index move? (one hour)

MDE: 3,205 seconds per alt; a hit rate 2 points off 50% is detectable.

| alt | same second: sign agrees / corr | next second: sign agrees / corr | after a big BTC second (>= 2 sd, n = 165): same-second agrees | next-second agrees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ETH | 78% / 0.77 | 55% / 0.18 | 99% | 63% |
| SOL | 82% / 0.69 | 59% / 0.25 | 99% | 72% |
| XRP | 73% / 0.55 | 59% / 0.33 | 95% | 75% |
| DOGE | 76% / 0.58 | 54% / 0.09 | 98% | 54% |
| ADA | 71% / 0.58 | 59% / 0.30 | 93% | 79% |
| BNB | 69% / 0.58 | 59% / 0.27 | 96% | 76% |
| HYPE | 69% / 0.54 | 58% / 0.25 | 93% | 72% |
| ZEC | 66% / 0.37 | 56% / 0.22 | 86% | 75% |
| BCH | 63% / 0.30 | 56% / 0.15 | 84% | 68% |
| NEAR | 60% / 0.27 | 54% / 0.08 | 71% | 60% |

Reverse check (alt now vs BTC next second): corr 0.03-0.09 for every alt,
so the direction is BTC -> alt, not the other way. Two seconds later: 50-54%,
i.e. gone. This agrees with the verifier's "1-s BTC->alt lead is real".
Same-second is the strong one; it is only usable if the bot sees BTC's print
before the alt's, which the bot does not (they arrive together) -- which is
exactly why the constituent feed matters: BTC's Bitstamp book is BTC's print
a second early.

## Q4 -- could the bot use this live?

- The bot already runs its index socket as a daemon thread
  (`IndexWS._run`, websockets + asyncio, ~35 lines). A `ConstituentWS` of the
  same shape connecting to Bitstamp's public `order_book_<coin>usd` channel
  (no key, 100-ms snapshots, all 8 feed coins in one socket) and optionally
  Coinbase's public `ticker` channel is ~150 lines plus tests. It keeps per
  coin a small ring of (local ms, mid) and exposes
  `move_against(coin, side, window_ms)`.
- Cost: crypto_feeds.py does the same job for four exchanges in 36 MB and
  ~135 messages/s; inside pinrun, one Bitstamp socket is ~10 msg/s per coin,
  well under what the 20 Hz loop already handles. Latency to our machine is
  72-85 ms.
- Where it sits: the entry path only, next to `fresh_level_block` (the one
  quote-age branch, pinrun.py ~line 16160), as a refuse-or-delay with a flag
  that ships OFF, a self-test proving it cannot touch the hedge pass, and a
  PREREG on live fills before it is turned on (the 2026-09-19 lesson: a gate
  must never block a hedge).
- If the feed dies: the rule must FAIL OPEN -- a stale feed (no quote in
  the last 2 s) means "no opinion", never "refuse", and the bot must keep
  trading exactly as today. That is a two-line check plus a self-test.
- Work: about a day including the startup-path test with the new flag
  applied (the --price-ceiling lesson). Not worth starting until Q3 says the
  signal separates our losers from our winners.
