## 1. F-C01 x F-C05 — is `880 sigma^2` the wrong variance, round the clock?

**Measure.** `Var(settle - strike) = 880 sigma^2` presumes 1-second index
increments are serially uncorrelated, and the bot hardcodes it
(`var_factor(r, [1.0])`, `pinrun.py:3792`); `engine.var_factor` already takes a
rho vector. Windows run 24/7, so quiet hours pool with busy ones. From the
1/sec index alone: estimate lag-1..k autocorrelation of its increments per
index and per clock block (weekday/weekend x 6 h), rebuild sd, compare against
the realised spread of `(settle - strike)/sd`. An sd 10% small makes a stated
1-in-1,000 nearer 1-in-400 — the shape of F-M04 and F-M08. Index tape only.

**Artefact if.** (a) The ruler, not the formula: sigma is a trailing 300 s
estimate, so re-run with hindsight sigma over the same 840 s; a ratio going to
1 is calibration s5's ground. (b) A quiet feed zero-inflates increments,
deflating sigma and faking autocorrelation — count repeated prints per window.
(c) Fat tails — report interquartile z. (d) Torn hours are not random on
the clock (F-I05) — print surviving closes per block.

**MDE / cost.** ~2,140 closes at ~1.22 independent each (F-R01) = ~2,600
effective: a variance ratio to ~+/-3%, so 5% mis-scaling shows and 2% is
no-power, not no-effect. Cheap — one index pass.

thin books — match resting size or it is depth. (d) Strictly backward of the
signal (F-O05).
