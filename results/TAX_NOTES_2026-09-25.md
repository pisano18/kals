# Kalshi taxes: what you owe depends on a question the IRS hasn't answered

*Written 2026-09-25. Covers everything Kalshi settled from Aug 15 to 3:03 AM ET Sep 25. Not tax advice. A CPA makes the final call.*

## The short version

- **Kalshi will not send you a tax form for these trades.** Its tax page (updated June 22, 2026) lists only forms for interest, rewards, and crypto transfers. You have to report the trading yourself. The record is ready: `results/tax/kalshi_2026_ledger.csv`, with one line for each of the **1,115 markets**.
- **So far in 2026 you made $460.26**, after $131.26 in trading fees. That matches Kalshi's own balance to within 4 cents ($584.46 deposited + $460.26 made = $1,044.72. Kalshi shows $1,044.68.)
- **The IRS has never said which tax rule covers these contracts.** Depending on the rule your CPA picks, 2026 so far counts as **$460, $567, or $1,529 of income**. That's up to 3.3 times what you actually made. At a 22% tax rate, the bill would be about **$101, $125, or $336**.
- **Nothing is late.** August lost $38.99, so there was no profit to pay on by the Sept 15 deadline. **The next deadline is Jan 15, 2027.** It covers everything from Sep 1 to Dec 31, which is +$499.24 so far.

## Why the same $460 can be three different incomes

The money you made on the markets you won: **$1,528.89** (on the markets that made money).
The money you lost on the markets you lost: **$1,068.63** (on the markets that lost money).
Together: **+$460.26**.

What counts as "income" depends on how the IRS classifies the contracts:

| How it's treated | What counts as income | 2026 so far | Tax at 12% | at 22% | at 24% | For it / against it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Capital gain** (many tax pros default to this) | wins minus losses | $460.26 | $55 | $101 | $110 | You buy something and it pays out or expires. Positions are held for minutes, far under a year, so the gains are short-term and taxed like wages. |
| **"Other income"** (a line on Schedule 1) | wins minus losses | $460.26 | $55 | $101 | $110 | Simple and cautious. One tax pro says no rule clearly requires it. |
| **Futures-style "60/40" rule** (tax code Section 1256) | wins minus losses, but 60% taxed at the lower long-term rate | $460.26 | — | **$82** (17.8% blend) | — | Kalshi is a federally regulated exchange, which is part of the definition. But the law excludes swaps, the IRS has never approved it, and Kalshi doesn't send the form futures brokers send. This is the **aggressive** choice. |
| **Gambling, and you itemize deductions** | wins minus **90%** of losses | $567.12 | $68 | $125 | $136 | A new law, starting 2026, lets gamblers subtract only 90% of losses. A bill to undo it was blocked in January 2026. |
| **Gambling, and you take the standard deduction** | **wins only**. Losses don't count at all. | **$1,528.89** | $183 | **$336** | $367 | The worst case. Losses only count if you itemize. |

If the gambling rule applies and each ET day counts as one "session" instead of each market counting on its own, wins are $833.66 on 14 up days and losses are $373.40 on 8 down days. That shrinks the worst case from $1,529 to $834. Whether the day or the market is the right unit is also unsettled.

If it's treated as a capital gain, the tax forms carry the full totals: **$44,406.84 paid back** against **$43,815.32 paid in**. Those look large, but only the difference, minus fees, is profit.

## What is settled and what isn't

**Settled (sources below):**
- Profits are taxable whether or not you get a form.
- Kalshi sends 1099-INT (interest), 1099-MISC (rewards/credits), and 1099-B / 1099-DA for **crypto transfers only**. None of these cover event-contract trades.
- For 2026, gambling losses are capped at 90%, and only up to your gambling wins. This is in the tax code at 26 USC 165(d). The IRS's own gambling page (updated Sep 24, 2026) still doesn't mention the 90% cap, but the statute does.
- The rules for paying during the year (below).

**Not settled:**
- Which of the treatments in the table applies. The IRS has published nothing on event contracts. Thomson Reuters reported this in June 2026, and a practitioner quoted there expects the question to reach the courts.
- Whether each market or each day is the unit that gets netted.
- Whether the 90% gambling cap gets repealed. The FAIR BET Act was blocked in January 2026, and a similar bill (HR 6985) is pending.
- Whether the **$8.14 in debit-card deposit fees** can be deducted. They are not trading fees and are not in the $131.26.
- How your state treats it.

## Paying during the year (to avoid an underpayment penalty)

The IRS splits the year into four periods, each with its own deadline:

| Period | Deadline | This account in that period |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 1 – Mar 31 | Apr 15, 2026 | not trading |
| Apr 1 – May 31 | Jun 15, 2026 | not trading |
| Jun 1 – Aug 31 | Sep 15, 2026 | **lost $38.99**, so nothing was owed on this income |
| Sep 1 – Dec 31 | **Jan 15, 2027** | +$499.24 so far |

- **No penalty applies** if you owe less than $1,000 at filing after withholding. It also doesn't apply if you paid at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000).
- **"Nothing owed Sep 15" depends on a worksheet.** Your CPA has to use the IRS's uneven-income worksheet (Form 2210, Schedule AI). Without it, the IRS assumes income came in evenly all year.
- **The easiest fix, if you have a paycheck:** raise the withholding at work before December (new W-4). The IRS counts withholding as if it were paid evenly all year, so extra taken out in November still counts toward earlier deadlines.

## State tax

This depends on your state, and it's usually on top of the federal tax. At 5% on the net, it's about $23 so far. **Some states never let gambling losses offset wins.** Practitioner lists disagree, but they name states such as Connecticut, Illinois, Indiana and Ohio. If the gambling treatment is used in one of those, the state would tax the full $1,528.89 of wins.

## Questions to take to a CPA

1. Which treatment for event contracts: capital, other income, 60/40, or gambling? Is the netting unit each market or each day?
2. If gambling: should I itemize so the losses count at all?
3. Is the 60/40 rule worth claiming, or too aggressive?
4. Are the debit-card deposit fees deductible?
5. Should I make a Jan 15, 2027 payment, or raise my W-4 withholding instead?
6. How does my state treat this?
7. Is "trader" tax status, or a mark-to-market election, worth looking at for 2027? These elections have early-year deadlines, so ask before spring.
