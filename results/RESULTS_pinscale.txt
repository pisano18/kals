==============================================================================
pinscale -- is the SCALE-IN RULE the problem, or is CONCENTRATION?
==============================================================================

THE HYPOTHESIS: 'the scale-in rule reads a collapsing price as a
discount and buys more.'  If true, LATER buys lose more often than
FIRST buys.  Everything below tries to refute that.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
POPULATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LIVE window tau 3-30s: 1,196 available trades, 83 closes,
    0 flips among 136 buys.
    ZERO flips -> every loss-rate question is 0/n here.  Unusable.

  WIDE window tau 3-60s (used for everything below):
    rows.jsonl CANNOT hold tau > 60: with more than 60s to run no
    settlement print is locked yet and pindata.partial() returns None.
    'tau up to 200' is pindata's argument default, not the data.
    tau 31-60 is a HARDER world -- the model is 3.7x overconfident at
    31-45s and 10.9x at 46-60s -- so rates here are ~4x live.  This is
    a STRESS population.  No number below is a live forecast.

Q1  LOSS RATE BY BUY INDEX -- does the scale-in rule buy worse trades?
==============================================================================

  MDE FIRST, before any estimate.  Comparing 'the first buy lost' over
  118 closes with 'any later buy lost' over 91 closes, at a base
  rate of 3.39%, one-sided alpha 0.05, 80% power:
    MDE = +8.8 percentage points (3.4% -> 12.2%, a 3.6x rise).
    ANYTHING SMALLER IS INVISIBLE HERE.  A doubling of the
    later-buy loss rate would NOT be detected by this sample.

  buy index   buys   flips   loss rate   closes   95% upper   mean price
      1        118       4      3.39%     118       7.59%       96.18c
      2         91       6      6.59%      91      12.60%       92.87c
      3         46       0      0.00%      46       6.30%       90.29c
      4         19       0      0.00%      19      14.59%       85.58c

  Clustered by close, ONE observation per close (hard rule 4):
    first buy lost             4 / 118 closes = 3.39%
    ANY later buy lost         6 /  91 closes = 6.59%
    difference               +3.20 pp   z = +1.08   cluster bootstrap 95% CI [-2.64, +9.55] pp

  MATCHED, on the SAME closes -- this separates 'later buys are worse'
  from 'closes that produce later buys are worse closes':
    of the 91 closes that produced a later buy,
      the FIRST buy lost on 3; a LATER buy lost on 6.

  VERDICT: NO DETECTABLE DIFFERENCE.  My hypothesis is NOT
  supported by this tape.  Given the MDE above that is 'no
  evidence', not 'no effect' -- a real effect smaller than the
  MDE would look exactly like this.

  drop bucket    buys  flips   loss rate   closes   mean price   median market/model

  'market/model' is how many times the MARKET's implied chance we lose

  one-sided z (big > small) = +0.13  -- not significant
  With 4 buckets, the multiple-looks threshold for alpha 0.05 is
  |z| >= 2.24 (Bonferroni), not 1.64.

Q5  WHY THE CAP RANKING MOVED: THE 3-LOSS BRAKE AND CAP 3 WERE NEVER
    TESTED TOGETHER
==============================================================================
  pinstress.simulate() halts on DOLLARS only (`realised <= loss_abort`).
  The loss-COUNT brake -- 3 losing trades, then stop -- was written
  into LOSS_PLAN.md and deployed AFTER that sweep ran.  Ablation on one
  population, so only the brake and the correlation change:

    brakes           correlation   cap   median $   5th %ile   halted
    $ only         ASSUMED       2   $  157.09  $   87.90     0.1%
    $ only         ASSUMED       3   $  193.71  $  104.71     0.9%
    $ only         MEASURED      2   $  158.90  $  100.20     0.0%
    $ only         MEASURED      3   $  193.71  $  123.71     0.0%
    $ + 3 losses     ASSUMED       2   $  157.09  $    6.85    27.0%
    $ + 3 losses     ASSUMED       3   $  108.02  $  -39.48    65.6%
    $ + 3 losses     MEASURED      2   $  158.90  $   10.44    31.7%
    $ + 3 losses     MEASURED      3   $   48.17  $  -36.41    84.4%

  With pinstress's brakes, cap 3 beats cap 2 -- this file REPRODUCES
  the number cap 3 was deployed on.  Add the brake that is actually
  live and the ranking reverses ON THIS POPULATION.

  *** AND NOW THE CHECK THAT NARROWS THAT CLAIM.  The wide window
  admits ~2x the buys per close, so it halts far more often than the
  live window would.  The SAME ablation on the tau 3-30 buy structure

  so the zero-flip problem does not bite: ***

    $ + 3 losses     MEASURED      2   $  117.29  $   15.99    13.4%
    $ + 3 losses     MEASURED      3   $  146.81  $   27.95    15.3%

  ON THE LIVE WINDOW CAP 3 STILL WINS THE MEDIAN, brake or no brake.
  So cap 3 is NOT refuted, and I am not going to say it is.  What the
  brake costs cap 3 is TAIL and UPTIME, not median profit.  The wide-
  window reversal above is driven by trade COUNT, not by cap 3 being
  a worse rule, and quoting it as 'cap 3 is wrong' would be an error.

  THE MECHANISM.  The brake counts TRADES; the unit of information is a
  CLOSE.  How many losing TRADES one losing CLOSE delivers:
    LIVE cap3 (any market)   {1: 6, 2: 2}
    cap3, 1 per market       {1: 8}

    cap 1: at most 1 losing trades from one close; 0 closes put every buy on one market
    cap 2: at most 2 losing trades from one close; 16 closes put every buy on one market
    cap 3: at most 3 losing trades from one close; 8 closes put every buy on one market
    cap 4: at most 4 losing trades from one close; 7 closes put every buy on one market

  LOSS_PLAN.md calls three losses 'roughly a 1% event' at the 0.90%
  rate.  That arithmetic treats three losing TRADES as three
  independent draws.  Under cap 3 with same-market repeats they can be
  ONE draw, which is what happened live: three fills on KXNEAR15M,
  three losing trades, the whole brake budget spent by a single close.

  THE COST OF ONE-PER-MARKET, in trades:
    refused 36 buys (2 of them losers); admitted 8 replacements (0 losers).
    net 26 WINNING trades given up for 2 losses avoided = 13 winners refused per loss avoided.
    profit destroyed: E@0.90% $230.44 -> $201.75 = -$28.69 over 118 closes = -$0.243 per close.

  AND WHERE THAT BENEFIT ACTUALLY COMES FROM -- it is the brake, not
  better trades.  With the loss-count brake switched OFF the two rules
  are within a few percent of each other:
    cap3 any market   brake OFF median $  193.71 (halt  0.1%)  |  brake ON median $   42.51 (halt 85.0%)
    cap3 1 per market  brake OFF median $  200.24 (halt  0.0%)  |  brake ON median $  200.24 (halt 34.5%)
  One-per-market's median is UNCHANGED by the brake, because it can
  never spend more than one unit of the brake budget on one close.

  TONIGHT AGAINST THIS SAMPLE.  The five worst closes in the 118:
    ['$-37.31', '$-33.19', '$-18.69', '$-18.13', '$-18.06']
    tonight, live: -$52.60.  Worse than every close in the sample, and
    88% of the $60 modelled worst case for one close.

==============================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEASURE
==============================================================================
  * tau 31-60 is not the live window.  Rates here are ~4x live and the
    RANKING of rules is what transfers, not the levels.
  * Only a handful of closes in this sample contain any loss at all, so
    every loss-rate comparison is underpowered by the MDE printed above.
  * pindata.Book.snapshot() reads yes_dollars/no_dollars where the tape
    carries yes_dollars_fp/no_dollars_fp, so the replayed books are
    delta-only and seeded empty.  That REMOVES offers, so this file
    sees fewer buying opportunities than really existed; it cannot
    invent one.  Conservative for opportunity count; whether it biases
    the loss RATE either way is NOT known and is not claimed here.
  * The race is not modelled.  26% of live orders fill nothing.
