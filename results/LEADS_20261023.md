One lead from 6 drawn pairs. **A PROPOSAL, not a result** -- this session holds
no market data and measured nothing.

## L1 -- re-clock the contest on the index print

**F-C02** settlement is the average of the sixty 1-second index prints before
the close. **F-O02** when somebody else takes the offer we wanted, the first
competing take lands at a median 67 ms, fastest tenth 6 ms.

**The measurement.** `pincontest` clocks that 67 ms from *the moment we would
have hit it* (its docstring). Nobody has clocked it from the index print.
Re-clock the 10,492 contested rows on `_rx_ms` of the last
`cfbenchmarks_value` frame and report the competing take's phase within the
second, inside the tau band we trade. A peak says the sweep is
**print-clocked**: it was coming at print+X whether or not our order existed,
so losing 28 races in 100 (F-P03) is a phase problem our own decision loop
controls and lead B / U38 can buy fills. Flat in print-phase but tight around
our decision moment says **order-clocked**: they react to us, phase buys
nothing, and lead B retires.

**Data.** `trade` + `cfbenchmarks_value`, plus pincontest's contested-row
cache. No order book, so no `load_quotes` and no 2-3 GB. One tape pass, cheap.

**Artefact if.** (1) Two clocks. `kalshi_collector.handle` stamps `_rx_ms` on
every frame from one clock in one process, so this is checkable, not fatal --
but `_rx_ms` is *our* receipt, so per-channel jitter smears a real peak toward
uniform. That biases to the null, the safe direction; cross-check any
exchange-assigned `ts` on the trade record. (2) Our own fills. Drop our
executions as pincontest does (4,749) or our own second-boundary habit (SH158)
makes the peak. (3) tau mixing: ~5% of trades are in the last 60 s (F-I03), so
a histogram over all tau is flow we never compete with. (4) No null: shuffle
each stamp uniformly inside its own second and require no peak.

**Smallest effect it can find, stated first.** 10,492 rows over ~716 closes at
rho ~0.8 is ~870 independent observations: it separates strong clocking (one
100 ms bin holding >=20% of the mass against a uniform 10%) from flat, and
cannot rule out weak -- a 12% bin reads as nothing. A null here is "no strong
clocking", never "no clocking".

**Not a loss rate.** The outcome variable is a timestamp. Nothing in it is our
loss rate and it may not be quoted as one (rule 5).
