## L1. Break-even is 3.58% because of a hard-coded entry price

**F-P01** (break-even 3.58% close-loss; win ~3.7c, loss ~96c)
x **F-X02** (taker fee `0.07*p*(1-p)`, billed per order, ceilinged).

`pinbank.break_even_rate()` = `win_per_close/worst_close`, where the win uses
`MEAN_PRICE = 0.961` hard-coded (`pinbank.py:48`) and assumes `MAX_PER_CLOSE = 2`
legs both win. F-P05 measures the live mean entry at **95.6c**. Measure two
numbers from the Kalshi ledger: the CONTRACT-weighted mean entry (not
per-market — scale-ins buy cheaper, late adds dearer, and they pull opposite
ways), and the mean winning legs per close (F-P03: 28.2% of orders fill nothing,
so 2 is a ceiling). Feed both into the same formula.

Re-running the repo's own arithmetic: 96.1c -> 3.58%, 95.6c -> 4.02%,
95.0c -> 4.56%. The constant is worth 0.4-1.0pp, and F-P02's two live rates
(2.86% current, 4.84% all-history) straddle exactly that range. Sign unknown
before measuring: a lower weighted entry raises break-even (we look better),
fewer winning legs lowers it. Both halves in one pass or it is half a
comparison.

**Needs:** `results/kalshi_ledger.json` + per-market fills. No tape, no replay.

**Artefact if:** hedge legs (entries near 50c) are counted into the mean entry
and drag it down; a hedged market writes TWO settled rows and is double-counted;
the bot's own logs are used instead of the ledger (they miss markets held when a
run died); the mean is taken over markets rather than contracts.

**Power, stated first:** this cannot settle whether we are above break-even —
separating a 4.02% break-even from a 4.84% realized rate needs ~5,000
independent closes (~130 days at 38/day) and we have 248. It fixes the
yardstick, not the verdict. **Cost:** an hour, arithmetic only.
