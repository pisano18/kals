# LEADS 2026-10-19

**A PROPOSAL. This box holds no market data and measured nothing.**


## 1. F-I03 x F-P06 — is the hedge-recovery decay liquidity, or the calendar?

- **F-I03** — 57.7M trade records over 376 h; only ~5% fall in the last 60 s of
  a quarter hour.
- **F-P06** — the hedge recovers about a third of a loss, decaying with size:
  34.9% at 20, 33.2% at 68, 22.3% at 250.

**Measure.** From the book alone, the hedge-side resting depth in the last 60 s
of a window, by second-to-close and by distance from the touch, with our own
orders excluded — a capacity curve in contracts. Then ask what share of it
each hedge size (20 / 68 / 250) must eat. If 250 is trivial
against that depth, the 34.9 -> 22.3 decay is NOT liquidity, and the likeliest
remaining explanation is the calendar: size follows the bank (F-B01/B02), so
the three size buckets are three different fortnights (F-M05 showed accuracy
moving exactly that way). Either answer changes something: a measured size
ceiling for the hedge, or the retirement of a decay we plan around.

**Needs.** `orderbook_snapshot` + `orderbook_delta` for tau < 60 s, plus our own
live hedge log for sizes and timestamps. One `load_quotes` job at a time (2-3 GB;
the collector outranks it). Medium cost; start on one coin, 5 days.

**MDE, before the estimate.** The book half is well powered: ~1,500 closes at
~1.22 effective each (F-R01), ample for depth percentiles. The realised half is
NOT: ~22-40 hedge events in all live history, split three ways, so a 12pp
recovery difference is not callable. Report that as no power, never as no
effect, and let the book half carry the conclusion.

**Artefact if.** (a) Resting depth is not obtainable depth — quotes pull when
crossed, so the curve is an UPPER BOUND on capacity and must never be converted
into a recovery rate for us (F-R05: the book says an offer was sitting there).
(b) Our own hedges are in that tape, and at 250 they are a material share of a
60-second stretch holding ~5% of all trades — exclude our order ids, and treat
the post-hedge book as contaminated by our own impact. (c) If depth is measured
only at the touch it will look thin everywhere; the hedge walks levels, so the
curve must be cumulative across the price band it would actually sweep.
