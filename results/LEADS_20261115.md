## L1 — F-M02 x F-O05: the leak curve is on the wrong clock

- **F-M02** — `sd/sigma` collapses far faster than `sqrt(tau)`; 9.7x too large at `tau = 10`.
- **F-O05** — Whether an offer gets taken is partly CAUSED by the outcome; the gap grows 3.18 -> 88.55 pp as the window widens 250 ms -> 60 s.

**The measurement.** F-O05's x-axis is window width in SECONDS. But F-M02/F-C05
say the answer leaks in proportion to settlement variance *consumed*, and at
fixed seconds that fraction moves enormously with `tau`: at `tau = 600` a
10-second window consumes a sliver, at `tau = 20` most of what is left. Re-plot
the leak against fraction-of-remaining-variance-consumed and ask whether
separate `tau` strata collapse onto one line. Deliverable: the widest window
staying under a stated leak, as a function of `tau`.

**Why it matters.** If 3.18 pp is a `tau`-average, the leak at the small `tau`
where we trade is worse than the headline, and every window width chosen off
that curve is contaminated exactly where it counts. A bug hunt (F-R04).

**Data.** Tape only: the 1/sec index (F-I01) for the clock, snapshot + delta for
when an offer rested, trades for when it was taken. Valid tape use — what the
index did, what the market did.

**MDE before the estimate.** Cluster by close time, never by market or offer:
rho 0.62-0.69, design effect 31-46x (F-R02). Report effective closes per
stratum; one that cannot resolve a difference under the 3.18 pp it is compared
against is "no power", not "no leak".

**Artefacts that would kill it.**
1. Censoring dressed as physics — a 60 s window cannot exist at `tau = 30`.
   Restrict to windows wholly inside the market's life; the collapse must hold
   in the overlapping region alone.
2. Pooled sigma sorts by coin (F-M09) — clock each window off its own prints.
3. Occupation-time selection — sample on a fixed `tau` grid, not on arrivals.
4. **Transport.** The population is the tape's, not ours (F-R05: 31x, intervals
   disjoint). Output is a window calibration and the TAPE's selection
   mechanism — never our loss rate, never a gate.

**Cost.** About a day. The clock is index-only and cheap (`pincalib.py` is the
pattern); the re-stratification reuses the contest machinery. `load_quotes`
holds 2-3 GB — one at a time.
