# When does the bot make its money? -- 2026-09-25

**Answer: no hour, weekday, weekend or news window makes or loses money differently
enough to size on. No time-based sizing schedule is proposed.** A schedule picked
from one week of the record and tried on the other week would have **lost $26**.

Source: Kalshi's own ledger (`results/kalshi_ledger.json`, written 3:03 AM ET
09-25), 15-minute crypto only, Sun 09-13 through 3 AM ET Fri 09-25: **649 markets,
459 traded quarter-hours, +$557.37 made on $38,947 bet = $1.43 per $100 bet,
17 losing markets.** Supply and fill numbers are from our live logs (1,118
watched quarter-hours, 848 buy orders). No tape or replay number is used.
Script: `python research/timeedge.py` (self-test passes).

## Why nothing can be sized on yet

- **Too little data per slot.** Each hour-of-day slot has only 11-28 traded
  quarter-hours. To be caught, an hour would have to make or lose about **$12.80
  per $100 bet** more than the rest of the day -- nine times the bot's whole
  edge. Day-of-week needs about $7.20 per $100; weekend-vs-weekday or stock-
  market-hours needs about $5.60 per $100. In a test world where one hour lost
  **5 times as often** as the rest, 14 days of data still did not clear the bar.
  Rough guess from that test world: about a month more of trading before a
  difference that large could be seen. A smaller one would take far longer.
- **Every red hour is one or two single losses, and most are from one day.** Sat
  09-19 (the day new gates blocked hedges) caused the losses in four of the five
  red hours:

  | hour (ET) | what made it red |
  |---|---|
  | 1-2 AM | two losses, both Sat 09-19 (-$57.76, -$62.28) |
  | 12-1 PM | Sat 09-19 (-$61.75) and Mon 09-21 (-$59.09) |
  | 3-4 PM | Sat 09-19 4:00 PM (-$107.95), Mon 09-14 (-$29.90) |
  | 8-9 PM | one loss, Wed 09-23 8:30 PM (-$130.41) |
  | 11 PM-12 AM | Sat 09-19 11:45 PM (-$108.86) |

- **Saturday = -$223, but the record holds only one Saturday (09-19).** Take that
  one day out and weekends made $4.20 per $100 bet against $2.07 on weekdays. The
  weekend-vs-weekday gap also points opposite ways in the two halves of the
  record (weekends better from 09-13 to 09-18, worse from 09-19 to 09-25).
- **The week matters more than the hour.** 09-13 to 09-18 made $3.09 per $100 bet,
  night and day alike. 09-19 to 09-25 made 4 cents per $100 (+$8.33 on $21,152),
  about break-even. Any hour pattern is buried under that swing.
- **Tried and failed:** midnight-9 AM ET looked better (+$2.26 per $100 vs $0.86 for
  the rest of the day), but only in the second half. In the first half it was a
  tie (3.22 vs 3.00 per $100). Chance, not an edge. (This one was picked after
  looking at the table, so it could not have counted anyway.)
- **The multiple-looks bar:** 12 slots had enough data to test. With that many
  comparisons, a difference only counts if it scores 3.01 or more on the test (a
  shuffled-time check set this bar; the textbook correction gives 2.87). The best
  real score was 2.98, for Saturday -- one day. Nothing else reached 2.

## The one steady time pattern: supply, not profit

- Between midnight and 9 AM ET, **16-22 of every 100 looks** find someone selling
  the side we want. From 9 AM to midnight it is **8-16 of every 100**. This ordering
  held in both halves of the record (they ranked the hours the same way at
  0.68, where 1 is identical and 0 is unrelated). That is why **43 of every 100
  trades happen in those 9 hours**.
- But those night trades do **not** make more per dollar (see the tie above).
  Nights give more trades, not better ones.
- Sizing up at night would mostly buy dearer offers. The cheapest offer typically
  holds 57 contracts at night and 50 by day, already below our size of 88. How
  much the dearer offers would cost was not measured here.

## The 7-10 PM slow window

- **09-19 to 09-25:** evening orders filled **67 of 100** vs **80 of 100** at other
  hours, and took 120 thousandths of a second vs 100. That matches the
  latency audit.
- **09-13 to 09-18:** the same evening hours filled **92 of 100**, better than
  other hours (87 of 100).
- So it is **not the hour itself**. Something that started around 09-19 made
  evenings slow. Orders at other hours also got slower in the second half (89 ->
  100 thousandths of a second), but evenings slowed the most. My guess (not
  measured): it is on our side -- this laptop's load in the evening, or the
  late index prints the collector received 09-19..09-23 -- not the market.
- Cost: about 10 missed fills in 6 evenings. At the record's average of about $0.86
  per traded market, that is **roughly $1-2 a day**. The fix is the order-speed
  work (reusing one connection per order), not a time schedule.

## Do the brakes fire at particular times? No -- they fire on bad days

- **Day-loss cap ($200 per ET day, in force since 09-20):** has never fired.
- **20% drawdown halt:** fired for real once, Sat 09-19 11:45 PM (it fired again
  on each restart until 12:30 AM). It also fired once falsely, Sun 09-13 11:18 PM: a
  bug read the account's high as $1,000,000.
- **Per-run $200 loss limit:** paused the bot 29 times, all on Sat 09-19,
  between 4:14 AM and 6:29 PM.
- **Open-position caps:** 6 pauses or halts on 4 days (Sun 09-13 4:44 PM, Thu 09-17 9:14 PM,
  Sat 09-19 11:44 PM, Wed 09-23 12:59 AM, 6:14 AM and 8:29 PM). They are scattered
  across the clock.

## What would change this

Re-run `python research/timeedge.py` weekly. It proposes a schedule on its own
only when a slot clears the multiple-looks bar AND points the same way in both
halves of the record. Most hour slots reach 30 traded quarter-hours (the
minimum it will test) around 10-02; the thinnest (10-11 AM, 10-11 PM) around
mid-October.
