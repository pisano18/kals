# Red team, 2026-09-25: what breaks the bot, and what it costs

**What this is.** 25 real-world failures planted into the bot's own trading
loop, run offline on fake markets. No order left this machine. Nothing live
was touched. Driver: `research/redteam.py` (self-test passes: it catches a
planted lost hedge and finds nothing in a world with nothing planted).

**How to read the dollars.** Every test world trades 5 contracts. Live trades
about 88 (size at a ~$1,043 bank). The "at live size" column is the test loss
times 17.6. That is arithmetic on a made-up market, NOT a measured loss. The
made-up market: we bought at 95c, it falls, and the hedge costs 72c, rising
2c every second it waits. The column is what that one failure costs once,
compared with the same world where nothing breaks. The worst case for a
position left with no protection is the whole thing: 88 x 95c = **$83.60 a
market**.

## The reds, biggest money first

**1. An order that went through but whose answer was lost (row 1).** Kalshi
takes the order, but the reply is lost (timeout, or a 500 from their gateway).
The bot marks it "unknown" and stops new bets. It still protects what it knows
about, then exits, and watch_bot restarts it within about a minute. Neither run
ever protects the unknown bet. Nothing reads our open positions from Kalshi at
startup (no `/portfolio/positions` call anywhere in pinrun). During an outage
this repeats: each restart can send one more order into the same hole. The
09-21/22 outage returned 388 timeouts. **I did not measure how many of those
were orders that actually filled.** That is the check to run next (Kalshi's
fills list against our logs for those hours).
*Fix (proposal):* at startup and after any "unknown", read our open positions
from Kalshi and add any 15-minute position the run doesn't know about to hedge
tracking. One call a second at most.

**2. A hedge that got a server error or no reply is treated as done (rows 2
and 3).** This is deliberate (K2, 09-22): re-sending a hedge that did go
through would double it. But nothing ever checks. Server errors come exactly
when markets are busy, and that is when hedges fire.
*Fix:* on the next pass, look the order up by its own id (or read our
position in that market). If it didn't fill, send it again. The double-hedge
worry goes away because we check before re-sending.

**3. Renamed book field (row 4).** `livebook.py` reads `yes_dollars_fp` /
`no_dollars_fp`. If Kalshi renames them, every book reads empty with no error.
One counter goes up (`snapshots_applied`), and nothing reads it. The bot can't
hedge ("no price", 9 times in 9 s) and quietly stops trading. The 1-minute
watch line keeps watch_bot happy, so nobody is told.
*Fix:* (a) when the held side shows no price, send the hedge anyway at the
emergency price. It is an order that can't sit on the book, so if there is
really nothing to buy it costs nothing. (b) A "blind" alarm to the phone when
every watched book has been empty for 60 s, or a market-list refresh loads
0 markets from a list that wasn't empty (that also covers row 12).

**4. New settlement status (row 5).** `reconcile()` books a result only when
status is exactly `finalized`. From memory (not checked this session),
Kalshi's API also lists `determined`, `disputed` and `amended`. A disputed
market can sit for hours.
*Fix:* book any yes/no `result` whatever the status word. Give an unknown
status a record and a phone alert, not a silent drop at 15 minutes.

**5. Run time ending mid-bet (row 6).** `while time.time() < end` just stops.
Every other halt winds down first.
*Fix:* treat the end of `--minutes` like any other halt, so it waits until
nothing is held.

## Ambers worth doing

- **The 20 s order timeout blocks everything (row 7).** `ordercli.py` waits up
  to 20 s for a reply, and one slow order freezes every hedge. *Fix:* with
  anything held near a close, cap the wait at a few seconds.
- **Hedging off a stale book (rows 8-10).** The hedge uses the book's price
  without checking how old it is. It sent 6 orders at a price that no longer
  existed. *Fix:* if the book is more than ~2 s old, use the emergency price.
- **Disk full (row 13).** The day-loss file failing to save turns the $200/day
  limit into $200 per restart. The recorders stop for good at 5 GB, long before
  the disk is actually full. Free disk now: **10.3 GB**.

## watch_bot / restart_bot, read (not run)

- Restart limit: 4 quick restarts in 15 min, then one every 3 min forever. A
  bot that crashes at startup costs trading time, not money.
- Money brakes wait 15 min before a restart. A drawdown halt waits for START on
  the app. The day limit restarts every 15 min and re-halts until midnight ET,
  as designed.
- **Loop to watch:** a stop for an "unknown order" is not a money brake, so it
  restarts at once. In an outage that is the row 1 hole on repeat.
- **Stale kill:** 25 min of log silence force-kills the bot even mid-bet. That
  can only happen live if log writes are failing (row 13).
- **Reboots:** idle sleep and idle hibernate are OFF (checked: never, on
  charger and on battery). Windows Update may still restart the laptop between
  9 AM and 3 PM ET (its quiet hours are 3 PM to 9 AM), and no update policy is
  set. After a reboot nothing restarts until you sign in, because the start-up
  task runs at sign-in. The last reboot was 09-15 9:32 AM ET. Nothing is
  pending now.
- **Calendar bomb, 2026-11-01:** `et_day_key()` hard-codes UTC-4. Once the
  clocks go back, the bot's "day" (and the $200/day limit) resets at 11 PM ET,
  an hour early.

## What was not tested

- Anything inside `livebook.py`'s or the index feed's own reconnect logic. The
  test replaces both with fakes.
- How often any of this has happened live. Every row here is a mechanism,
  not a count.
